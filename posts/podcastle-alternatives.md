# Podcastle alternatives: what each one replaces, now that it is Async

**Bottom line:** if you record remote interviews and want recording, editing, AI voices and hosting
in one place, nothing on this list replaces Podcastle (now Async) more completely than staying on
it; **Riverside** and **Descript** are the closest all-round alternatives. If what you actually want
is to tidy up your own voice by editing a transcript, with an export that contains only words you
really said, **Paraphrase** does that one job and costs less than Async's paid plans, but it does far less overall.

Something to know before you compare anything: **Podcastle is now called Async.** Visiting
podcastle.ai redirects to async.com, and Async's podcast page is headed "Formerly Podcastle". The
recording studio, audio editor and hosting are still there, now inside a broader product that Async
describes as a "Chat-based AI creative suite for video content production", with AI video and image
generation and a credit system on top. We checked all of this on Async's own pages on 17 October
2026. If you are looking at alternatives because the product you signed up for has changed shape,
that is a perfectly good reason to look.

## Why people look for Podcastle alternatives

**The product has widened.** Async describes its podcast studio as the "same end-to-end studio you
knew and loved, now part of Async". Async's plans lead with AI credits
for models like Kling, Veo and Seedance, chat-based video editing and image generation. If you only
ever used the recorder and the audio editor, much of what the plans now advertise is not for you.

**The free plan is a trial in practice.** Async's free plan allowances are lifetime, not monthly: 2
hours of audio transcription, 1 hour of remote multitrack recording and 10 AI credits. Audio export
is up to 160kbps MP3, the export limit is "1 file", and its comparison table does not include Magic
Dust, noise reduction or silence removal on Free. That is enough for trying it, and not a plan you
can keep producing a show on. We break this down in [what Podcastle costs](/blog/podcastle-pricing).

**Transcription covers five languages.** Async's help centre lists English, Spanish, German, French
and Italian for transcription, which matters if you edit by transcript in any other language.

**Some people want less AI voice, not more.** Async offers over 1,000 AI voices and, on Pro and
above, voice cloning from a few seconds of audio. That is a feature for many people. For others, a tool that cannot put
words in their mouth is the point.

## Like-for-like alternatives to Podcastle

### Riverside

The closest match for Async's remote recording studio. Riverside's own
site says it captures "up to 4K video and uncompressed audio in separate tracks, unaffected by
internet connection", much the same promise Async makes for its studio. It also has a
text-based editor where you "search, cut, copy, and paste right in the transcript", and it generates
transcriptions, titles and chapters.

**Best for:** shows with remote guests where recording quality per speaker is the priority.

### Descript

A text-based editor for video and audio. Descript describes itself as an editor where "you cut, trim, and
rearrange the video by changing the transcript", with filler words and retakes cut "in a click".
Like Async, it can also generate voice audio, video, music and images to fill gaps. If what you
liked about Podcastle was Text Mode, Descript is the like-for-like move.

**Best for:** people who edit video and audio mostly through the transcript and are happy to use
generated audio.

### CapCut

A video editor first. CapCut's site leads with an "AI-Powered Video Editor for Everyone", automatic
subtitles, voice enhancement and text to speech. It overlaps with the video side of Async rather
than the podcast side.

**Best for:** short-form and social video, where the podcast studio was never the draw.

## Narrower tools that do one part well

### Cleanvoice

Cleanvoice removes filler words and long silences from a recording automatically, handles multitrack
edits "always in sync", and lets you "export your timeline to any editor". It is an add-on to your
workflow rather than a whole studio. Its site offers 30 minutes free.

**Best for:** people who already have an editor they like and want the cleanup step done for them.

### Audacity

Audacity is free and open source, and runs on Windows, macOS and Linux. It is a waveform editor you
install, not a browser studio, so it replaces Podcastle's audio editor rather than its recorder or
hosting.

**Best for:** anyone who wants full manual control and pays nothing.

### Paraphrase

Paraphrase is what we make, so weigh this accordingly. It does one thing: you record or upload, it
transcribes every word with a timestamp, and you edit by clicking words to cut them or dragging them
to reorder. The export is your original recording with pieces removed, cut at word boundaries. One
button, [Clean up with AI](/docs/ai-cleanup), proposes the cut for you: filled pauses, false
starts, hedges and repetition, including the same point made twice in different words, judged in
context rather than from a word list. Every proposed cut stays on screen struck through, so you can
put any word back.

It **never generates speech**. No AI voices, no cloning, no text to speech. A word you never said
cannot appear in an export, because there is no audio for it.

It transcribes 25 European languages, detected automatically. Premium is £13.99 a month (about £4.99
a month on the three year cycle) and includes 120 hours of audio a month; the free plan gives every
account 15 minutes of transcription with no card.

It is **not a Podcastle replacement** for most podcasters. There is no remote recording studio, no
separate per-speaker tracks, no speaker labels, no noise removal or levelling, no music, no
captions, no hosting and no collaboration. If you rely on any of those, you would keep a tool for
them and use Paraphrase only for the edit, or not use it at all.

**Best for:** solo shows, voiceovers and narration where the job is cutting your own stumbles out.
There is more on that in [podcast editing with Paraphrase](/podcast-editing).

## Do you actually need to replace Podcastle?

Often not. If you use the recording studio with remote guests, the hosting hub and the audio cleanup
together, Async still does all of that in one browser tab, and moving means a new recorder, a new
editor and a new host. That is a lot of change to avoid some features you can simply ignore.

Switching makes sense if you were only using a slice of it (the editor, say) and the plans cost more
than that slice is worth to you, if you need transcription in a language outside Async's five,
or if you want a tool that structurally cannot generate your voice.

Our [Podcastle review](/blog/podcastle-review) covers what it does well, and
[Paraphrase vs Podcastle](/blog/paraphrase-vs-podcastle) compares the editing approach directly.

The quickest way to judge is on your own audio. Upload a recording you have already edited
somewhere else to a [free Paraphrase account](https://paraphrase.bot), press Clean up with AI, and
compare. Fifteen minutes of transcription are included, no card needed.
