# Paraphrase vs Descript: a full suite versus editing only your real voice

**Bottom line:** Descript does far more: video, remote recording, noise clean-up, captions,
translation and AI voices. If you use any of that, choose Descript. **Paraphrase** does one job,
cutting your own recording by its word-timed transcript, with a one-button AI cleanup you review
and no generated speech at all. Its credits are spent only at transcription, so re-editing is
free. If all you do is tidy spoken audio, Paraphrase is the simpler tool for it.

Descript is one of the best-known tools for editing audio by its transcript, and it does a great
deal more besides. This comparison is about where the two take different approaches, and it tries
to be straight about how much more Descript does.

## What the two have in common

Both transcribe your recording and let you edit it by editing the words. Descript says it plainly:
"Text-based editing lets you edit video and audio by editing the transcript." In Paraphrase you
click a word to cut it, shift-click to cut a run, and drag a word to move it, and the export is
your recording with those pieces taken out, with cut words left on screen, struck through, so you
can bring them back.

Both also offer an AI pass that tightens a recording for you and lets you review it, which is
where the interesting differences start.

## Cleaning up a recording

**Descript** has two tools here. Remove filler words underlines fillers like "um" and "uh", lists
each one with a timestamp and preview, and lets you delete it, leave a gap, or strike it through.
Descript's help centre says it "currently detects filler words in English transcripts only" and
uses AI credits. Edit for Clarity goes further: it "analyzes your script to clean up pacing,
tighten delivery, and remove unnecessary or off-topic content" at Low, Medium or Heavy intensity,
and keeps cuts as ignored text you can restore. It also uses AI credits.

**Paraphrase** has one button. A language model reads the transcript and proposes the cut: filled
pauses, false starts, hedges, repetition (including the same point made twice in different words),
and it can move words where a sentence reads better. There is no word list; it judges each word in
context and keeps anything that carries meaning. The cuts show struck through and any word can be
put back. It has been tested on English speech, so we do not claim cleanup quality for other
languages. It uses no credits; instead it is limited to 3 a day on the free plan and 30 a day on
Premium. [How AI cleanup works](/docs/ai-cleanup) has the detail.

Honestly, these are close in spirit. The difference is less in the cleanup than in what can happen
after it.

## Generated speech: the real difference

Descript offers AI speech as part of editing. Regenerate, paired with a custom voice, can
"generate text-to-speech audio to fix a flubbed delivery, a misspeak, or a wrong spoken word", and
Edit for Clarity's "Reword for flow" option can "regenerate the audio with AI speech so the edits
sound seamless". Descript gates this well: a voice clone needs "explicit recorded authorization"
from the speaker, Regenerate needs an authorised custom speaker, and generated edits are shown in
blue. It is opt-in and genuinely useful if you want it.

**Paraphrase never generates speech.** No voice cloning, no overdub, no text to speech, no
regenerated words. A word you never said cannot appear in an export, because there is no audio
for it. That is a design decision rather than a missing feature: if you edit interviews, testimony,
journalism or a client's own voice, you can hand over the export knowing every word in it was
spoken.

## Pricing

Descript's paid plans are $16, $24 or $50 per person a month billed annually ($24, $35 or $65
monthly), with monthly media hours and AI credits that "don't roll over into the next cycle".

Paraphrase Premium is £13.99 a month, £83.85 a year or £179.65 for three years (about £4.99 a
month). Its 120 hours a month also reset and do not roll over. **One credit transcribes one second
of audio, and that is the only thing credits are spent on.** There is a free plan, a 7 day Premium
trial on the monthly and annual cycles, and credit packs that never expire while your account is
open.

The two are in different currencies, so we have not converted them. The full breakdown is in

## What Paraphrase does not do

Plainly, so nobody switches expecting more:

- No noise removal, enhancement, levelling, EQ, music or sound effects.
- No remote recording studio, multitrack, or separate per-speaker tracks.
- No speaker labels, captions, subtitles or translation.
- No collaboration, comments or share links.
- No screen recording, no desktop or mobile app, no public API.

If any of those is part of how you work, Descript (or Descript plus something else) is the honest
answer.

## So which should you use?

**Choose Descript** if you make video, record remote guests, need captions or translation, want
Studio Sound, work in a team, or would use AI voice to patch a line. It is a full production suite,
and our [Descript review](/blog/descript-review) covers it in more depth.

**Choose Paraphrase** if you record yourself (a [podcast](/podcast-editing), a
[voiceover](/voiceover-editing), a [course](/course-editing), an [interview](/interview-editing))
and want a clean cut of your real voice with as little effort as possible: one cleanup button, a
proposal you review, lossless export, and no chance of a generated word.

**Use both** if you like Descript for production but want the spoken edit done somewhere that
cannot generate speech. Paraphrase exports WAV or FLAC, which you can bring into whatever you use
next. If you are still weighing other tools, see [Descript alternatives](/blog/descript-alternatives).

The quickest way to decide is on your own recording. [Make a free account](https://paraphrase.bot),
no card needed, and you get 15 minutes of transcription to try it.
