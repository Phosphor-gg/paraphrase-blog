# Podcastle review: a full podcast studio, now inside Async

**Bottom line:** Podcastle, now Async, is one of the most complete browser podcast studios there is:
remote recording on separate tracks, a timeline and transcript editor, filler word and silence
removal, noise cleanup, AI voices and hosting, all in one place. Its trade-offs are a free plan with
lifetime limits, transcription in five languages, filler detection from a preset list, and a product
that is increasingly about AI video. If you only need to tidy your own voice, a narrower tool may
suit you better.

We make Paraphrase, a narrower editor, so treat this as an interested party's assessment. **We have
not used Podcastle or Async hands-on for this review.** Every claim below comes from Async's own
product pages, pricing page and help centre, read on 17 October 2026, and we link the relevant ones.
Where the documentation does not say, we say so rather than guess.

## What Podcastle is now

Podcastle has been renamed **Async**. Podcastle.ai redirects to async.com, the podcast page is headed
"Formerly Podcastle", and the help centre still calls the app "Async Creator" in places. The
podcast studio sits inside a larger product that Async describes as a "Chat-based AI creative suite
for video content production", with AI video, image, music and avatar generation paid for in
credits. The podcast tools are all still documented, and that is what this review covers.

## What Podcastle does well

**Remote recording is the headline, and it is well specified.** Async says its studio records "4K
video and uncompressed WAV audio, captured as clean, separate tracks for every speaker", recorded
locally on each guest's machine so a shaky connection does not ruin the take. Guests join from a
link, up to 10 participants per session, and there is a teleprompter, Producer Mode, branded waiting
rooms and an iPhone app that can record remote interviews too. You can download separate or combined
tracks afterwards. For interview shows, this is the part most alternatives are measured against.

**You can edit by transcript or on a timeline.** Text Mode transcribes your tracks, with "separate
lines for each recording participant", and lets you click a word to remove it from "both the
transcript and the audio file". There is a separate Correct action that fixes a transcript typo
without touching the audio, which is a sensible distinction to make explicit. When you need finer
control, the multitrack timeline is still there, with fades, speed control and volume.

**Filler words and silences are one click each.** The Filler tab detects a preset list (mmm, um, uh,
uhm, uhh, umm, mm, mhm) and lets you remove them individually or all at once. The Silences tab finds
pauses longer than 0.5 seconds by default, with an adjustable threshold, and Silence Removal can
also run on a whole project from the AI Assistant. A Words search lets you remove every instance of
any word you choose. For video, a newer Rough Cut tool removes filler words, retakes and unnecessary
silences in one pass.

**The audio cleanup goes further than editing.** Magic Dust is Async's one-click enhancer that
"clears away background noise, evens out distracting volume spikes, and polishes your voice", with
separate noise reduction and auto-levelling tools documented in the help centre. Magic Dust, noise
reduction and silence removal are listed on every paid plan.

**AI voices are there if you want them.** Over 1,000 voices in 15 languages, multi-speaker
narration, and voice cloning from a short sample. Async's own suggested uses for podcasters are
intros, outros, sponsor reads and segment bridges "without re-recording".

**Publishing is built in.** The Hosting Hub gives you an RSS feed and a podcast page, so a show can
go from recording to published without leaving the browser.

**Export is flexible on paid plans.** The help centre lists MP3, WAV, AIFF, FLAC, AAC, WMA, AC3 and
OGG for audio and SD to 4K for video. Paid plans export audio at up to 320kbps MP3 or WAV.

## Where it may not suit you

**The free plan is lifetime, not monthly.** 2 hours of audio transcription, 1 hour of remote
recording and 10 AI credits for the life of the account, with audio export up to 160kbps MP3 and an
export limit of 1 file. Fine for evaluating, not for publishing every week. Paid plans start at
£19.99 a month, or £11.99 a month billed yearly; we cover the detail in
[Podcastle pricing](/blog/podcastle-pricing).

**Transcription is listed for five languages.** English, Spanish, German, French and Italian,
according to a help article dated January 2026. If you record in Dutch, Polish or Swedish, the text
editor may not be available to you. Async asks people to email with language requests.

**Filler detection is a preset list.** The documented Filler tab looks for specific sounds. It does
not, according to the documentation, look for false starts, hedges, or the same point made twice.
Rough Cut adds retakes on video, and we could not find documentation of how it decides what counts
as one. We have not tested either.

**The documentation does not describe reordering words.** Text Mode documents removing, correcting
and highlighting words. We found nothing about dragging a word or phrase to a different place in the
transcript. That may exist; we could not confirm it.

**The product's centre of gravity has moved.** The pricing page now leads with credits for video
models like Kling, Veo and Seedance. If you came for a podcast studio, you are paying for a plan
designed around a broader product, and credit costs for chat-driven tasks "can depend on what the AI
actually ends up doing", in Async's own words. Hour limits for transcription and recording are
separate and predictable, which helps.

**Generated speech is part of the toolkit.** For many people that is a feature. If you want an
editor that cannot put a word you never said into your episode, this is not that, by design.

## What we could not verify

We did not find the free trial length, the price of top-up credit packs, or which tick-box features
(including text-based editing and voice cloning) are on the free plan, on Async's pages. We also did
not find a date for the rename. We have left all of that out rather than guess.

## Where Paraphrase fits, and where it does not

Paraphrase only does the editing part, and in a different way:

- **It never generates speech.** No AI voices, cloning or text to speech. Your export is your
  original recording with pieces cut out, so every word in it is one you said.
- **One cleanup button that reads context.** [Clean up with AI](/docs/ai-cleanup) proposes cutting
  filled pauses, false starts, hedges and repetition, including a point repeated in different words,
  with no word list. Every cut is shown struck through and can be put back.
- **You can move words**, not just remove them: drag a word, or use Shift and the arrow keys.
- **25 European languages**, detected automatically.
- **£13.99 a month with 120 hours of audio**, or 15 minutes free with no card.

Where it does not fill the gap, plainly:

- **No remote recording studio, no multitrack, no separate per-speaker tracks.**
- **No noise removal, enhancement, levelling, music or effects.** Nothing like Magic Dust.
- **No speaker labels, captions, subtitles or translation.**
- **No hosting, collaboration, comments or share links.**
- **AI cleanup is tested on English speech.** We do not make claims for cleanup quality in other
  languages.

There is a [direct comparison of the two](/blog/paraphrase-vs-podcastle) if you want the editing

## Who Podcastle is right for

Podcastle is right for you if you run an interview show with remote guests and want recording,
editing, sound cleanup and publishing in one browser tab, or if you use AI voices for intros and
reads. For that workflow it is one of the most complete tools available, and much of what it does
Paraphrase does not do at all.

It may not be the right fit if you record yourself, mostly need to cut your own stumbles, record in
a language outside its five, or want a tool that cannot generate your voice. That is a description of
what it is built for rather than a criticism.

If that second group sounds like you, try the edit on your own recording with a [free Paraphrase
account](https://paraphrase.bot). Fifteen minutes of transcription, no card.
