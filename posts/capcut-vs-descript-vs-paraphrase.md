# CapCut vs Descript vs Paraphrase: editing talking-head video by its transcript

**Bottom line:** if you want one app that takes a clip to a finished, captioned, styled short on
your phone, pick **CapCut**. If you want a desktop editor built around the transcript, with screen
recording, Studio Sound and Edit for Clarity, pick **Descript**. Pick **Paraphrase** when the slow
part is cutting the talking itself: its AI cleanup proposes the cut of false starts, hedges and
repetition for you to review word by word, it exports an MP4 with picture and sound cut at the same
words, and it never generates speech. Many people will use Paraphrase first and one of the others
after.

## What each one is for

**CapCut** is a complete video editor on desktop, mobile and the web: templates, effects,
transitions, auto captions, background removal, motion tracking, keyframes, video translation and
AI video generation. Its transcript-based editing lets you delete text to delete video, and
"text corrections carry into the caption track", which matters for short-form where captions are
close to expected.

**Descript** is an audio and video editor you drive by its transcript. Alongside captions it lists
screen recording, Eye Contact, Green Screen, Create Clips, Studio Sound, translation and dubbing,
Underlord (an AI co-editor that takes text instructions) and Rooms for remote recording.

**Paraphrase** does only the cut. Every word in your recording gets a timestamp, and every edit is
a choice of which of your words stay and in what order. Upload a video and the sound is taken out
in your browser and uploaded on its own; the picture is only uploaded if you export MP4.

## Where they overlap

All three transcribe your clip and let you delete words to delete footage. The difference is how
much of the deciding each does for you.

- **CapCut's** desktop filler workflow turns on "Identify filter words" in Auto captions and
  highlights candidates such as "um", "uh", "like" and "you know"; you "choose whether to delete or
  manually adjust them". It also documents automatic removal: "With the 'Remove filler words' option
  enabled, CapCut instantly cleans your audio", and in transcript editing it "removes them with one
  click". Its own advice is to "Treat highlighting as a shortlist, not a verdict." Its documented
  examples are fillers and pauses; one page says it helps clean "hesitations or repeated phrases",
  and we found nothing about catching the same point made twice in different words. Whether filler
  removal needs Pro is unclear from CapCut's own pages, so check in the app.
- **Descript's** Remove filler words lists each one with a timestamp and preview and works on
  English transcripts only. Edit for Clarity cleans "up pacing" and removes "unnecessary or
  off-topic content" at Low, Medium or Heavy intensity, keeping cuts as text you can restore. Both
  use AI credits.
- **Paraphrase's** Clean up with AI has a language model read the transcript and propose cutting
  filled pauses, false starts, hedges and repetition, including the same point made twice in
  different words, and moving words where a sentence reads better. There is no word list. Cuts stay
  on screen struck through and any word can go back. It has been tested on English speech. See
  [AI cleanup](/docs/ai-cleanup).

We have not tested all three on the same clip, so this compares approaches, not results.

**Your own words only.** CapCut's overdub uses "AI voice cloning technology" to replace "incorrect
audio with accurate voice-over by editing the transcript" (available in some regions), and it has
text to speech. Descript's Regenerate types new words into an authorised voice clone. Both are
useful. Paraphrase **never generates speech**: the export is your original recording with pieces
cut out, so a word you never said cannot appear. If you publish under your own name, that guarantee
comes from the tool, not from your discipline.

**Paraphrase** Premium is £13.99 a month, £83.85 a year or £179.65 for three years (about £4.99 a
month), with 120 hours of transcription a month and 30 cleanups a day (3 on the free plan). Credits
are spent only at transcription, so re-editing and re-exporting are free. See [pricing](/pricing).

## What Paraphrase does not do

For video, this list matters:

- No captions, subtitles, translation or speaker labels.
- No effects, templates, transitions, music or sound effects.
- No noise removal, levelling or speech enhancement.
- No mobile or desktop app, no screen recording, no collaboration or share links.
- An audio-only take shows as black in an MP4 export.
- Projects up to 5 minutes of recordings on the free plan, 4 hours on Premium.

## Which should you pick?

**You make shorts and edit on your phone:** CapCut. Captions that follow your edits, plus effects
and templates, on desktop, mobile and the web. Our [CapCut review](/blog/capcut-review) goes deeper.

**You make tutorials, screen recordings or longer video:** Descript, with screen recording, Studio
Sound and Edit for Clarity in one editor. See our [Descript review](/blog/descript-review).

**Your clips ramble, and finding the restarts is the slow part:** Paraphrase for the cut, then
CapCut or Descript for captions and polish. See [short-form video](/short-form-video) and
[video editing](/video-editing).

**You must never publish a word you did not say:** Paraphrase, which cannot generate one.
