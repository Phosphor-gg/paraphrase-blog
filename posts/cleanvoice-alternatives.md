# Cleanvoice alternatives: the honest options for cleaning up speech

**Bottom line:** if you want the same hands-off job (upload, remove ums, mouth clicks, breaths and
noise, download), none of the tools below is a like-for-like replacement: each is a different kind
of tool, so switching mostly buys you a different workflow. We have not tested how their clean-up
sounds against Cleanvoice's. If what you actually want is to **decide which
words stay**, cutting false starts and repeated points rather than sounds, a transcript editor such
as **Paraphrase** does that job, and it pairs with Cleanvoice rather than replacing it.

Cleanvoice does one thing very deliberately: it takes a recording, finds the sounds that make
speech messy, and gives you back a cleaner file. People looking for alternatives are usually after
one of three things, and only some of them are solved by switching tools.

## Why people look for Cleanvoice alternatives

**They want to see and choose every edit.** Cleanvoice's own description of the flow is upload,
"turn on filler-word removal and start", and "you download the edited file within minutes". After
processing it shows a report of what was removed and lets you compare the original with the edited
version. If you want to change individual edits, the documented route is exporting markers or
timestamps into Audition, Premiere, DaVinci Resolve, REAPER, Audacity or anything that reads an EDL,
and adjusting them there. For a lot of people that is ideal. For people without a desktop editor it
is a step they would rather not take.

**They want to edit by what was said.** Cleanvoice says plainly that it "doesn't work based on the
transcript-to-edit flow", and its filler detection "works on how speech sounds, not on text". That
is a strength for accents and many languages, and it also means it is listening for sounds, not
judging whether a sentence said the same thing twice.

**They want one application for everything.** Cleanvoice concentrates on cleaning and cutting
speech. People who want to record, arrange, caption and edit video in one place end up looking at
all-in-one editors.

**They are comparing prices.** We have covered [what Cleanvoice costs](/blog/cleanvoice-pricing),
including how its rollover credits work.

## Like-for-like alternatives

### Descript

A text-based audio and video editor. Its site lists Remove Filler Words, Studio Sound, Remove Retakes,
captions, translation and remote recording, and it also offers AI speech, including voice clones
and stock AI voices. If you want a single application for recording and editing, and
you are comfortable with generated speech being part of the toolset, it covers more ground than
Cleanvoice does.

**Best for:** people who want one editor for everything.

### Riverside

Primarily a recording platform: it records audio and up to 4K video for each guest in separate
tracks, recorded locally so the internet connection does not affect them, then gives you a
text-based editor and AI audio clean-up on top. If your problem starts at recording, with guests on
bad connections, Riverside solves something Cleanvoice was never built
for.

**Best for:** interview shows that record remotely.

### Podcastle, now Async

podcastle.ai now redirects to async.com, which calls itself "Formerly Podcastle". Its page
describes a video editor you instruct ("Tell Async what to cut, pace, or polish") that removes
"filler, pauses, repeats, bad takes", plus audio clean-up, a transcript-based audio editor,
captions and clips, and it says "AI can make mistakes. Please review all edits." It leans towards
video.

**Best for:** creators who want an AI to do the rough cut of a video.

### CapCut and Audacity

CapCut is a video editor with noise reduction, voice enhancement and auto captions among its
tools. Audacity is the free, open source desktop audio editor for Windows, macOS and Linux, with
plugin support and a built-in Noise Reduction effect. Both are free to start and suit you if you
will do the editing yourself.

**Best for:** doing it by hand, on video (CapCut) or audio (Audacity).

## What you will struggle to replace

Worth being straight about this. **Cleanvoice does a lot of sound work in one automatic pass.** It
removes
lip smacks, saliva crackle and mouth clicks, breaths (muted or reduced), hum, hiss, wind, traffic
and room reverb, and it can balance loudness, all in the same pass as the cuts. It shortens long
pauses by context (a thinking pause gets shorter, a change of topic keeps a longer one), and it can **mute edits instead of cutting
them** so a video timeline stays in sync. It handles up to five tracks together, transcribes in 99
languages, writes summaries and show notes, and has an API.

Paraphrase does none of the sound work. It has no noise removal, enhancement, levelling or EQ, and
because every edit is a choice of which of your words stay, it has no mouth sound or breath
remover. If clicks and room noise are your problem, keep Cleanvoice.

## If the problem is the words, not the sounds

Some recordings are clean and still too long, because the speaker started a sentence three times,
made the same point twice in different words, or hedged every claim. That is a judgement about
meaning, and it is the job Paraphrase is built for.

Paraphrase is what we make, so weigh this accordingly. You record or upload, and the recording is
transcribed with a timestamp on every word. You edit by clicking words to cut them (click again to
restore), and you can drag words to reorder them. One [Clean up with AI](/docs/ai-cleanup) button
has a language model read the transcript and propose a cut: filled pauses, false starts, hedges and
repetition, including the same point made twice in different words. Every proposed cut shows struck
through, and you can put any word back. The export is your original recording with pieces cut out,
in WAV, FLAC, MP3, M4A or Opus, or MP4 for a video. It never generates speech, so nothing in the
export is a word you did not say.

It is **not a Cleanvoice replacement**. Transcription covers 25 European languages, and the cleanup
has been tested on English speech. It has no speaker labels, captions, multitrack or public API.
Premium is £13.99 a month with 120 hours of audio included; the [pricing page](/pricing) has the
annual and three year options.

**Best for:** running before or after Cleanvoice. Cut the words in Paraphrase, then clean the
sound in Cleanvoice, or the other way round.

## Do you actually need to replace Cleanvoice?

Usually not. If your recordings come back sounding right and you rarely change an edit, Cleanvoice
is doing its job, and none of the tools above is built to do that same hands-off job.

Switching makes sense if you want a full editor (Descript, CapCut, Audacity), if your trouble starts
at recording (Riverside), or if you want to decide every cut yourself by reading what you said
(Paraphrase). There is a [side by side comparison](/blog/paraphrase-vs-cleanvoice) of the last of
those, and our [Cleanvoice review](/blog/cleanvoice-review) covers what it does well in more detail.

The quickest test is your own recording. [Create a free account](https://paraphrase.bot) (no card,
15 minutes of transcription), upload an episode you have already cleaned, and see what the cleanup
proposes cutting from the words.
