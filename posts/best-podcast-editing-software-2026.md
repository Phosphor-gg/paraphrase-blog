# The best podcast editing software in 2026

**Bottom line:** if your show has remote guests, **Riverside** covers the most of the job, from
recording separate local tracks to editing by transcript to hosting and publishing. **Podcastle
(now Async)** is the closest alternative, with hosting included. **Descript** is the strongest
editor of the three if you also make video. **Cleanvoice** is the best add-on for automatically
stripping fillers, mouth sounds and noise from finished files, and **Audacity** is the best free
option if you are happy editing a waveform. For a solo or narrated show where you want to cut your
own words by transcript and be certain nothing in the edit was generated, **Paraphrase** (ours) is
built for exactly that and nothing more.

Podcast editing is really four jobs: getting a clean recording, cutting it, cleaning the sound, and
getting it out to listeners. Most tools are strong at one or two. This list ranks them on **how
much of that chain each one handles well**, so a narrow tool ranks lower even when it is the best
at its one part. We say where that happens.

## 1. Riverside: record, edit and publish in one place

Riverside's strength is the recording. Its help centre says high-quality tracks "are recorded
locally on each participant's computer or device" so "slow internet should not affect their
quality", with a separate track per person. The editor then lets you cut by transcript, remove
filler words and pauses, remove background noise, normalise audio, add music and captions, and
export your timeline to Premiere Pro or Final Cut Pro. Pro adds podcast hosting and analytics, and
publishing to YouTube, Spotify, Apple and others.

Two things to read carefully. Separate-track downloads are capped by plan (15 hours a month on Pro
according to its plan cards, though a table on the same page says 5). And its filler removal is
listed on "All plans" in the help centre but presented as a Pro feature on the pricing page.

**Best for:** interview and panel shows with remote guests. Our [Riverside review](/blog/riverside-review)
has the detail.

## 2. Podcastle (now Async): the same shape, with hosting

Podcastle now redirects to Async. Its studio records "separate audio and video tracks for every
guest locally", up to 10 participants, with an iPhone app for remote interviews. Text Mode edits
multitrack audio from the transcript, a Filler tab finds "um, uh, mhm" and similar for removal one
by one or all at once, silence removal defaults to gaps over half a second, and Magic Dust "clears
away background noise". The Hosting Hub gives you "an RSS feed and a dedicated podcast page".

Its free plan's limits are lifetime rather than monthly, and transcription is listed in five
languages. The Essentials plan card says 4 hours of recording while the comparison table says 2,
so check before relying on either.

**Best for:** podcasters who want recording, editing, clean-up and hosting under one login. See
our [Podcastle review](/blog/podcastle-review).

## 3. Descript: the strongest editor, especially with video

Descript ranks third here only because we could not confirm hosting. As an editor it is the most
complete: transcript editing for audio and video, multitrack transcription with speaker detection,
Studio Sound for noise and echo, filler-word and retake removal, captions, and Rooms for remote
recording with a separate file per participant. Edit for Clarity can tighten a whole episode at
the intensity you choose and keeps the cuts as "ignored text" you can restore.

It also generates speech if you want it: Regenerate and voice clones, with a recorded consent
step. Filler-word detection works on English transcripts only.

**Best for:** video podcasts and anyone who wants one editor for everything. Read our
[Descript review](/blog/descript-review).

## 4. Cleanvoice: the best automatic clean-up

Cleanvoice is not an editor in the usual sense. You upload, it processes, you download. It removes
filler words, stutters, hesitations, mouth sounds, breaths and dead air, cleans background noise
and reverb, and normalises loudness. Its filler detection "works on how speech sounds, not on
text", across more than 20 languages, and it can edit up to five tracks in sync. It also writes
show notes, chapters and summaries.

To change an individual edit, its documented route is exporting markers or timestamps to Audacity,
Audition, Reaper or another editor. Its own advice is worth repeating: "Not every um has to go."

**Best for:** batch-cleaning finished episodes, especially in languages other than English. See our
[Cleanvoice review](/blog/cleanvoice-review).

## 5. Paraphrase: cutting a solo show by its words

Paraphrase is ours, so weigh this accordingly. It ranks fifth on this list's axis because it
covers one link in the chain: cutting. On that link it leads in three specific ways.

- **Every AI cut is reviewable word by word.** One button proposes cuts for filled pauses, false
  starts, hedges and repetition, including the same point made twice in different words. Each one
  shows struck through in the transcript and any word can be restored. See
  [how AI cleanup works](/docs/ai-cleanup).
- **It never generates speech**, so what listeners hear is what you said.
- **Re-editing is free.** Credits are spent once, at transcription; editing, cleanup and export
  use none.

What it does not do matters for podcasts: no remote recording, no multitrack or speaker labels, no
noise removal, no hosting. It works best on a single recording of one voice, or a recording you
are happy to cut as one track. More on [editing podcasts with Paraphrase](/podcast-editing).

**Best for:** solo, narrated and scripted shows recorded in one take or several.

## 6. Audacity: the best free option

Audacity is free and open source, and its homepage says it "will always remain free for everyone".
It records and edits on the waveform with unlimited undo, and its Cut feature page names "Remove
ums, coughs, and long pauses from spoken recordings" as a podcast use. Noise Reduction works best
on steady noise like hiss and hum, and it has a compressor and loudness normalisation to a LUFS
target. You do every cut by hand, which is slow but completely in your control.

**Best for:** anyone on no budget who is comfortable on a waveform. See our
[Audacity review](/blog/audacity-review).

## 7. CapCut: for turning episodes into clips

CapCut is a video editor rather than a podcast tool, but if your show is filmed it is useful
downstream: transcript-based editing, auto captions, and a "Long video to short videos" tool for
social clips. Its pages do not make clear whether filler removal needs Pro.

**Best for:** cutting filmed episodes into short-form video. See our [CapCut review](/blog/capcut-review).

## How to choose podcast editing software

- **Guests on the call?** Start with the recording: Riverside or Podcastle (now Async), or
  Descript's Rooms.
- **Filmed episodes?** Descript for the edit, CapCut for the clips.
- **Noisy rooms, lots of ums, no time?** Run the files through Cleanvoice.
- **No budget?** Audacity, and accept the time it takes.
- **Solo show, and you care exactly which words stay?** Paraphrase, possibly alongside one of the
  above for recording or sound.
