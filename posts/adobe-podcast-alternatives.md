# Adobe Podcast alternatives: the honest options for enhancing and editing speech

**Bottom line:** if you want what Enhance Speech does (upload a voice recording, get it back with
the noise and echo gone), the alternatives that clean the sound are **Descript**, **Riverside**,
**Podcastle (now Async)**, **Cleanvoice**, **CapCut** and **Audacity**, and we have not tested how
any of them sounds against Adobe's. If you want an AI to **clean up the words**, cutting filler,
false starts and repeated points rather than noise, **Paraphrase** does that job, and it pairs with
Adobe Podcast rather than replacing it.

Adobe Podcast does two things well: Enhance Speech makes a voice sound better, and Studio records
guests and edits by transcript, all in the browser and free to start. People looking for
alternatives are usually after one of four things, and not all of them are solved by switching.

## Why people look for Adobe Podcast alternatives

**Premium is no longer sold on its own.** Since 30 August 2026, new customers get Adobe Podcast's
paid features through Adobe Firefly Pro, shown at $19.99 a month on podcast.adobe.com and £19.99 a
month including VAT on Adobe's UK Firefly page (checked 17 October 2026). It bundles Firefly
generative credits and Adobe Express too. That is good value if you want those, and more than some
people want to pay for podcast editing. We have covered
[what Adobe Podcast costs](/blog/adobe-podcast-pricing) in detail.

**They want filler words handled for them.** Adobe's pages describe Studio as a transcript you edit
by deleting, cutting and pasting text, and say it "can help you easily identify filler words" so you
can decide which to cut. They do not describe automatic filler removal, a pause shortener, or an AI
that suggests cuts.

**They need more languages or platforms.** Studio transcribes seven languages and runs only on
desktop Chrome.

**They want a full editor.** Studio is labelled beta and is built around the transcript. People who
want a timeline, effects or AI voices end up looking at larger editors.

## Alternatives that also clean the sound

### Descript

A text-based audio and video editor: "Deleting a word removes that moment of media". Its Studio
Sound "enhances spoken voice by reducing background noise, echo, and other distractions", and Remove
Filler Words lists each detected filler with a preview so you can delete or ignore it, in English
transcripts. It also offers AI speech, including voice clones, which some people want and others
specifically do not.

**Best for:** one editor for everything, including generated speech.

### Riverside

Primarily a recording platform, recording each guest locally in separate tracks, with a text-based
editor on all plans. Its help centre describes filler word detection with "Remove all", one-click
pause removal, background noise removal and Magic Audio, and a Find Fluff feature on paid plans that
suggests segments to remove. It transcribes around 100 languages.

**Best for:** interview shows that record remotely and want more automation in the edit.

### Podcastle, now Async

podcastle.ai now redirects to async.com, which describes itself as "Formerly Podcastle". Its audio
editor has a Text Mode for editing by transcript, automatic filler detection with remove-all,
silence removal and Magic Dust, its audio enhancer. It also offers over 1,000 AI voices and voice
cloning.

**Best for:** a recording and editing suite with AI voices included.

### Cleanvoice

Cleans recordings by sound rather than by transcript: fillers, stutters, mouth sounds, breaths, long
silences, background noise and echo, in one automatic pass. Its filler removal covers 20 or more
languages. You upload, process and download; to change individual edits it documents exporting
markers to a desktop editor.

**Best for:** hands-off clean-up of lots of files.

### CapCut and Audacity

CapCut is a video editor with transcript-based editing, filler word detection, noise reduction and a
voice enhancer, plus text to speech. Audacity is the free, open source desktop audio editor for
Windows, macOS and Linux, with a built-in Noise Reduction effect; you edit the waveform by hand.

**Best for:** social video (CapCut), or full manual control at no cost (Audacity).

## What you will struggle to replace

Worth being straight about. **Adobe Podcast's free plan is unusually generous**: an hour of Enhance
Speech every day and Studio projects up to 30 minutes, renewing daily, in a browser. Its paid tier
adds video enhancement, source separation with separate speech, music and ambience stems, and
two hour files. Studio records remote guests to separate WAV tracks, detects speakers, captions
video, exports SRT subtitles and makes audiograms. Finding all of that free, in one tab, is hard.

Paraphrase does none of the sound work. It has no noise removal, enhancement, levelling or EQ, no
remote recording, no captions and no music. If noise is your problem, keep Enhance Speech.

## If the problem is the words, not the sound

Some recordings sound fine and are still too long, because the speaker started a sentence three
times, made the same point twice in different words, or hedged every claim. Deleting those by hand in
a transcript works, but it means reading every line looking for them.

Paraphrase is what we make, so weigh this accordingly. You record or upload, and the recording is
transcribed with a timestamp on every word. You click words to cut them (click again to restore) and
drag words to reorder them. One [Clean up with AI](/docs/ai-cleanup) button has a language model
read the transcript and propose a cut: filled pauses, false starts, hedges and repetition, including
the same point made twice in different words. Every proposed cut shows struck through, and you can
put any word back. The export is your original recording with pieces cut out, in WAV, FLAC, MP3, M4A
or Opus, or MP4 for a video. It never generates speech.

It is **not an Adobe Podcast replacement**. It transcribes 25 European languages, and the cleanup has
been tested on English speech. Premium is £13.99 a month with 120 hours of audio included; the
[pricing page](/pricing) has the annual and three year options.

**Best for:** running alongside Enhance Speech. Enhance the sound in Adobe Podcast, cut the words in
Paraphrase, or the other way round. It works for [podcasts](/podcast-editing),
[interviews](/interview-editing) and [voiceovers](/voiceover-editing).

## Do you actually need to replace Adobe Podcast?

Often not. If your recordings come back from Enhance Speech sounding right and you are happy
deleting filler words by hand, Adobe Podcast is doing its job, and the free plan may be all you need.

Switching makes sense if you want a full editor with filler removal built in (Descript, Riverside,
Podcastle, CapCut), a hands-off clean-up by sound (Cleanvoice), manual control for free (Audacity),
or an AI that proposes cuts by meaning while you keep the final say (Paraphrase). There is a
[side by side comparison](/blog/paraphrase-vs-adobe-podcast) of the last of those, and our
[Adobe Podcast review](/blog/adobe-podcast-review) covers what it does well in more detail.

The quickest test is your own recording. [Create a free account](https://paraphrase.bot) (no card,
15 minutes of transcription), upload a take you have already enhanced, and see what the cleanup
proposes cutting from the words.
