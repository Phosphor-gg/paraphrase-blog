# Cleanvoice vs Descript vs Paraphrase: cleaning the sound, the suite, or the words

**Bottom line:** pick **Cleanvoice** if your recordings sound rough: it removes fillers, stutters,
mouth clicks, breaths, dead air, noise and echo automatically, across several tracks and in many
languages. Pick **Descript** if you want one editor for everything, transcript editing, video,
Studio Sound, captions and AI voices. Pick **Paraphrase** if your recordings ramble: its AI cleanup
reads the transcript and proposes cutting false starts, hedges and repeated points, which you review
word by word, and it never generates speech. Plenty of people should pair Paraphrase with
Cleanvoice.

## What each one is for

**Cleanvoice** cleans spoken audio in the browser. You upload, pick a template of fixes, and download
the result. Its own FAQ says "Cleanvoice doesn't work based on the transcript-to-edit flow", and
filler detection "works on how speech sounds, not on text", so it "also copes with accents and with
languages that sound similar". It still transcribes your file, in 99 languages. It also removes
background noise and room reverb, balances loudness, can mute edits instead of cutting so video stays
in sync, writes summaries and show notes, and has an API.

**Descript** is an audio and video editor you drive by its transcript, with captions, screen
recording, Studio Sound, Remove Filler Words, Edit for Clarity, Regenerate, translation and Rooms for
remote recording.

**Paraphrase** edits the words. Every word gets a timestamp, and every edit is a choice of which of
your words stay and in what order. The export is your original recording with pieces cut out at word
boundaries.

## Where they overlap

All three remove filler words. They differ in what else they find and how you check the result.

**What gets found.** Cleanvoice finds things a transcript cannot show: stutters, "short hesitation
sounds", lip smacks and breaths, and it adds room noise where a filler was "to make the flow of the
podcast more natural". Descript's filler tool works on English transcripts, and Edit for Clarity
removes "unnecessary or off-topic content". Paraphrase's [Clean up with AI](/docs/ai-cleanup) reads
the transcript and proposes cutting filled pauses, false starts, hedges and repetition, including
the same point made twice in different words, and can move words where a sentence reads better. It
has been tested on English speech. Clicks and breaths do not show up in a transcript, and Cleanvoice's
pages do not describe finding false starts or a point made twice in different words; on repeated
words its own pages disagree, one listing "repeated words" and its help centre saying that feature
is "yet to go live".

**How you review.** Cleanvoice shows a report and lets you compare the original with the edited
version. To change individual edits, its documented route is exporting markers or timestamps into
Audition, Premiere, DaVinci Resolve, REAPER, Audacity or any EDL editor; we have not used the app
hands-on. Descript lists each filler with a timestamp and preview, and keeps Edit for Clarity's cuts
as ignored text you can restore. Paraphrase leaves every cut on screen, struck through, and one click
puts any word back, in the browser.

**Generated speech.** Descript's Regenerate (in an authorised custom voice) and Edit for Clarity's
"Reword for flow" can generate AI speech. Paraphrase **never generates speech**, so a word you never said
cannot appear in an export.

We have not run the same recording through all three, so this compares approaches, not results.

## What Paraphrase does not do

- No noise removal, enhancement, levelling, EQ, music or effects.
- No stutter, mouth sound or breath removal, and no silence remover.
- No multitrack, speaker labels, captions, subtitles or summaries.
- No remote recording, collaboration or public API.
- No AI voice, cloning, overdub or text to speech.

## Which should you pick?

**Your recordings sound rough (a noisy room, clicks, breaths, lots of ums across many episodes):**
Cleanvoice. Our [Cleanvoice review](/blog/cleanvoice-review) covers it.

**You want one editor for audio, video, captions and remote guests:** Descript. See our
[Descript review](/blog/descript-review).

**Your recordings ramble, restart and repeat themselves:** Paraphrase, for a
[podcast](/podcast-editing), [voiceover](/voiceover-editing) or [course](/course-editing) where you
want to choose every cut by reading it.

**Both problems:** cut the words in Paraphrase, export lossless WAV or FLAC, then run it through
Cleanvoice for the sound.

**You record in a language outside Paraphrase's 25:** Cleanvoice or Descript.

The pairwise posts are [Paraphrase vs Cleanvoice](/blog/paraphrase-vs-cleanvoice) and
[Paraphrase vs Descript](/blog/paraphrase-vs-descript).
