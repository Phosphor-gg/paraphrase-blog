# Descript review: what it is good at and where it runs out

**Bottom line:** Descript is a very broad text-based editor: audio and video, remote
recording, noise and echo clean-up, captions, translation and AI voices, all driven from a
transcript. Where it runs out is mostly the other side of that breadth: two separate monthly
allowances that do not roll over, filler word detection and Regenerate that are English only, and
AI speech offered inside features some people would rather keep away from their voice. If you use the
breadth, it is a lot of tool for the price. If you only cut spoken audio, a narrower tool may suit
you better.

We make Paraphrase, a narrower tool in the same space, so treat this as an interested party's
assessment. We have not used Descript hands-on for this review. Every claim below comes from
Descript's own pricing page, feature pages and help centre, read on 17 October 2026.

## What Descript is

Descript is an audio and video editor built around the transcript. In its own words, "Text-based
editing lets you edit video and audio by editing the transcript." Around that it has Underlord (an
AI co-editor you instruct in text), captions, screen recording, Eye Contact, Green Screen, Create
Clips, translation and dubbing, Studio Sound, Remove Filler Words, Remove Retakes, Regenerate,
Rooms for remote recording, templates and integrations.

It runs as a desktop app on macOS 14 or Windows 11 and later, and in Chromium-based browsers
(Chrome, Edge and Opera are officially supported). Descript says it is not officially supported on
mobile browsers or tablets.

## What Descript does well

**The scope is the point.** It lets you record a remote guest, transcribe both tracks with
speaker detection, cut the conversation by editing text, clean the sound, add captions, and export
up to 4K video (on Creator and above), without leaving the app. If you make video podcasts or
talking-head content, that integration is the reason to use it.

**Rooms is a proper remote studio.** Descript describes it as "a remote recording studio that runs
in your browser" where "everyone records locally in up to 4K" and "every participant gets a
separate file", uploaded during the session with a cloud backup. That design protects you from a
guest's bad connection.

**Filler word removal is reviewable.** Descript underlines detected fillers in the script, then
lists each one with its timestamp and an audio preview. For each you can delete it, delete and
leave a gap to keep timing, or strike it through in the script while removing the audio. An "Avoid
harsh cuts" setting skips fillers that cannot be removed without "clipping into nearby words or
leaving behind an awkward cut". That is a careful piece of design.

**Edit for Clarity goes further than fillers.** It "analyzes your script to clean up pacing,
tighten delivery, and remove unnecessary or off-topic content", at Low, Medium or Heavy intensity.
Cuts are kept as ignored text so you can review and restore them, which is the right way to present
an AI edit.

**Studio Sound is aimed at rough recordings.** It "enhances spoken voice by reducing background
noise, echo, and other distractions", and Descript suggests turning it on "when a recording sounds
noisy, echoey, or like it was captured on a laptop mic".

**Voice cloning is gated properly.** A custom voice needs "explicit recorded authorization from the
person whose voice will be used", Descript will not clone a deceased or non-consenting person, and
using Regenerate to change what was said needs an authorised custom speaker, not a stock voice.

**Free to try properly.** The Free plan needs no card and includes text-based editing, an hour of
media a month and two hours of Rooms recording.

## Where Descript runs out

**Two allowances, and neither rolls over.** Paid plans include media hours (10, 30 or 40 a month on
Hobbyist, Creator and Business) and AI credits (400, 800 or 1,500). Media hours count uploads and
recordings "regardless of whether they're transcribed", AI credits are spent by Studio Sound,
Remove filler words, Edit for Clarity, Underlord and AI speech, and "Unused Media minutes and AI
Credits don't roll over into the next cycle." Descript says the cost of a tool you apply yourself
scales with the size of the job, caps Studio Sound and Remove Filler Words at 30 credits per file
each time you apply them, and says Underlord has "no fixed price per action", so how far 400 credits
go depends on what you run. Our [Descript pricing breakdown](/blog/descript-pricing) has the full plan table.

**Filler word detection and Regenerate are English only.** The filler words article says: "This
feature currently detects filler words in English transcripts only." Regenerate "only supports English audio" and is
limited to 250 characters per action. Transcription handles one language per file.

**AI speech sits close to the edit.** Edit for Clarity has a "Reword for flow" option that can
"regenerate the audio with AI speech so the edits sound seamless", with generated edits shown in
blue. It needs an AI speaker assigned to each speaker. But if your work depends on every word in the export being something you actually said (journalism,
testimony, a client's own voice), you have to keep track of which features generate and which do
not.

**Audio export is M4A, WAV or MP3.** The audio export article lists ".m4a, .wav, or
.mp3". FLAC and Opus are not among them.

**The free plan is for trying, not shipping.** Free exports are 720p with a watermark, the 100 AI
credits are one-time, and the AI tools are marked "Limited".

**Breadth is more than some people need.** If you only need one of these tools, you are working
around the rest.

## Where Paraphrase differs, and where it does not

Paraphrase does one part of what Descript does: you edit your own recording by its word-timed
transcript, and the export is that recording with pieces cut out.

- **It never generates speech.** No voice cloning, no overdub, no text to speech. A word you never
  said cannot appear in an export.
- **One-button cleanup, as a proposal.** A language model proposes cuts for filled pauses, false
  starts, hedges and repetition, including the same point made twice in different words, and can
  reorder words. Every cut shows struck through and can be put back. It has been tested on English
  speech; [how cleanup works](/docs/ai-cleanup) explains it.
- **Credits are spent once, at transcription.** One credit is one second. Editing, cleanup and
  exporting use none, so re-editing is free. Cleanup is limited by count instead: 30 a day on
  Premium.
- **Five audio formats, two of them lossless.** WAV and FLAC (lossless), MP3, M4A and Opus.

Where it does not fill the gap, plainly:

- **No noise removal or enhancement.** Nothing like Studio Sound.
- **No remote recording studio, multitrack or speaker labels.** Nothing like Rooms.
- **No captions, subtitles or translation**, no screen recording, no collaboration or comments.
- **Video is cut, not edited.** If a project has a video take, Paraphrase exports MP4 with picture
  and sound cut at the same words, and that is the extent of it.
- **Browser only.** No desktop app.
- **Priced in pounds:** Premium is £13.99 a month or about £4.99 a month on the three year cycle,
  with 120 hours of transcription a month.

## Who Descript is right for

Descript is right for you if you make video as well as audio, record remote guests, need captions
or translation, work in a team, or want noise and echo clean-up in the same place you edit.

It is less of a fit if you only cut your own spoken audio, or want a tool where generated speech
cannot appear at all. That is a description of what a broad suite is for rather than a criticism, and plenty of people will
reasonably use Descript for everything.
