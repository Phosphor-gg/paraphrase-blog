# Paraphrase vs CapCut: editing talking-head video by its transcript

**Bottom line:** if you want one app that takes a talking-head clip to a finished, captioned,
styled short, use **CapCut**; Paraphrase does not make captions, effects or templates. If the slow
part is cutting the talking itself, the false starts, repeats and hedges, **Paraphrase** proposes
that cut for you to review, exports an MP4 with picture and sound cut at the same words, and never
generates speech. Many people should use both, Paraphrase first.

These are not the same kind of tool, so this comparison is less about which is better and more
about which job each one is for.

## What CapCut does better

Most of it, if you are making finished social video.

**It is a complete editor.** Templates, effects and filters, transitions, sound effects, auto
captions, background removal, motion tracking, keyframes, green screen, video translation and AI
video generation. Paraphrase has none of these.

**Captions follow your transcript edits.** CapCut says "text corrections carry into the caption
track". For short-form video, where captions are close to expected, that may matter more than
anything else in the table.

**It is free to start** and many people never need Pro, as long as their project avoids premium
elements. Our [CapCut pricing](/blog/capcut-pricing) post goes through what triggers the Pro
prompt.

## How each one cuts the talking

This is where they genuinely differ.

**CapCut** transcribes the clip and lets you delete text to delete video. For filler words, its
desktop workflow turns on "Identify filter words" in Auto captions, highlights candidates like
"um", "uh", "like" and "you know", and you decide whether to delete or adjust each one. CapCut also
says it "may also trim pauses". Its own advice is to "Treat highlighting as a shortlist, not a
verdict." Its pages describe filler words and pauses; we did not find them describing false
starts or repeated points.

**Paraphrase** treats every edit as a choice of which of your words stay and in what order. Click a
word to cut it, click again to put it back, Shift-click to cut a run, drag a word to move it. The
**Clean up with AI** button has a language model read the whole transcript and propose a cut:

- filled pauses such as um and uh,
- false starts,
- hedges,
- repetition, including the same point made twice in different words,
- and moving words where a sentence reads better.

There is no word list. It judges each word in context and keeps anything that carries meaning.
The result is a proposal: every cut stays on screen struck through, and one click restores any of
it. Cleanup is tested on English speech. See [AI cleanup](/docs/ai-cleanup) and
[editing](/docs/editing).

Neither approach removes the need to watch it back. The difference is how much of the deciding is
done for you before you start.

## Picture and sound, cut together

For talking-head video, a transcript edit is only useful if the picture follows the words.
Paraphrase's MP4 export is your original recording with pieces taken out, nothing added. If you
upload a video, the sound is taken out in your browser and uploaded on its own, and the picture is
only uploaded if you export MP4. See [exporting](/docs/exporting).

CapCut keeps your footage in a full timeline, so the cut video is simply part of the project you
go on to caption and style.

## Your words, and only your words

This is the deliberate difference.

CapCut's overdub tool uses "AI voice cloning technology" to "generate a precise and accurate
version of your voice", replacing "incorrect audio with accurate voice-over by editing the
transcript". It also has text to speech and AI voices. Those are real features many creators want.

**Paraphrase never generates speech.** No voice cloning, no overdub, no text to speech. A word you
never said cannot appear in an export, because there is no audio for it. That is a design
decision, not a missing feature. If you publish under your own name and want every word in the
video to be one you actually said, that guarantee comes from the tool rather than from your
discipline.

## What Paraphrase does not do

To be straight about it:

- No captions or subtitles, no translation, no speaker labels.
- No effects, templates, music, sound effects or transitions.
- No noise removal, levelling or speech enhancement.
- No mobile or desktop app, no collaboration or share links.
- Cleanup tested on English only.
- Free plan projects are capped at 5 minutes of recordings; Premium projects at 4 hours.

## Using both

For short-form, the workflow that plays to each tool's strength is simple:

1. Record or upload the take in Paraphrase and run the cleanup.
2. Review the proposed cuts, put back anything you want, move a sentence if it reads better.
3. Export MP4.
4. Bring it into CapCut for captions, effects, music and the export your platform wants.

You lose nothing CapCut does, and the talking is already tight before you open the timeline. More
on this at [short-form video](/short-form-video) and [video editing](/video-editing).

## Why trust this comparison

Everything above about CapCut comes from CapCut's own site, all read on 17 October 2026: the pricing
page, help articles on what Pro costs, monthly and yearly plans, the free trial, cancelling,
exporting, watermarks and the Pro export prompt, and the tool pages for transcript-based editing,
filler word removal and AI overdub. We have not tested CapCut hands-on.

We make Paraphrase, so this is not a neutral comparison. What we have done instead is bold CapCut's
wins in the table, give its strengths their own section, and leave out what we could not read.

CapCut's own pages disagree in several places, and we have not picked a side. The transcript
editing page says "100+ languages" while its FAQ lists four, so we give no language count. The
pricing page says 1 TB of storage on Pro and a help article says 100GB. A CapCut blog article
quotes Pro in dollars at figures that do not match the pricing page, which itself says displayed
prices are "for reference only" and vary by region, so we quote only the pounds we were shown. And
whether filler word removal needs Pro is unclear: one page says it is free "with CapCut's trial",
another calls transcript editing free, and the blog article says auto captions, where the filler
workflow starts, is Pro only. Check in the app.

**Where Paraphrase leads, and where it does not.** It never generates speech, so an export can only
contain words you said. Every AI cut is shown in the transcript and can be restored word by word.
Credits are spent only at transcription, so cleanup, re-editing and re-exporting are free. It is
also by far the narrower tool: no remote recording, no noise removal, no captions, no AI voice and
no collaboration.

We have no customer reviews, ratings or testimonials to show you, because we have not collected
any. Judge it on one of your own clips instead.

## So which should you use?

Use **CapCut** on its own if you want captions, effects and templates in one app, on your phone as
well as your desktop, and your clips are short enough that cutting the talking by hand is quick.

Use **Paraphrase first** if the talking is what takes the time: long takes full of restarts and
repeated points, where you would rather review a proposed cut than make every one yourself. Then
finish in CapCut. There is more in our [CapCut review](/blog/capcut-review) and our list of
[CapCut alternatives](/blog/capcut-alternatives), and the Paraphrase plans are on the
[pricing page](/pricing).

The fairest test is one of your own clips. [Make a free Paraphrase account](https://paraphrase.bot),
run the cleanup on a talking-head take, and put the MP4 side by side with what you would have cut
in CapCut.
