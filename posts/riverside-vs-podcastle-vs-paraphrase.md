# Riverside vs Podcastle vs Paraphrase: two recording studios and one editor

**Bottom line:** if you interview remote guests, choose between **Riverside** and **Podcastle (now
Async)**; Paraphrase records no guests. Riverside suits you if you want live streaming, webinars,
transcription in around a hundred languages and publishing to YouTube, Spotify, Apple and others.
Podcastle suits you if you want a library of over 1,000 AI voices, a timeline and transcript editor
and hosting with an RSS feed. Pick **Paraphrase** if you record alone and the edit is the job: one AI cleanup you
review word by word, words you can drag into a new order, free re-editing, and no generated speech.

## What each one is for

**Riverside** is a remote recording studio and content platform. Tracks "are recorded locally on
each participant's computer or device", so "Slow internet should not affect their quality." Pro adds
up to 4K video and 48kHz audio, a teleprompter, podcast hosting and publishing to YouTube, Spotify,
Apple and others. Grow adds Full HD live streaming, multistreaming and social scheduling, and Webinar
adds webinars with registration and Q&A.

**Podcastle** has become Async: podcastle.ai redirects to async.com, whose podcast page is headed
"Formerly Podcastle". Its studio records "clean, separate tracks for every speaker" in up to 4K video
and WAV audio, with a teleprompter, an iPhone app and, on Teams and above, Producer Mode. It edits on a multitrack
timeline or in Text Mode, cleans sound with Magic Dust, generates voices, and hosts the show with "an
RSS feed and a dedicated podcast page".

**Paraphrase** only edits. You record yourself in the browser or upload a file, every word gets a
timestamp, and every edit is a choice of which of your words stay and in what order. The export is
your original recording with pieces cut out at word boundaries.

## Where they overlap

All three let you cut by removing words from the transcript. Riverside shows removed text "in gray
with a strikethrough" and can mute words instead of cutting them. Podcastle's Text Mode removes words
"from both the transcript and the audio file". Paraphrase leaves cut words on screen, struck through,
and one click restores any of them.

The automatic cleanup differs.

- **Riverside** groups "um", "uh" and "hmm", contextual fillers like "you know" that are "flagged
  only when used as a filler", and breaths, each to accept or discard. A slider removes pauses. On
  paid plans, Find Fluff suggests segments such as "redundant takes, chatter, and technical
  discussions". Its help centre lists filler removal on all plans while its pricing page presents it
  under Pro, so check your plan.
- **Podcastle** finds a preset list ("mmm, um, uh, uhm, uhh, umm, mm, mhm") and silences over half a
  second by default. Rough Cut, for video, removes fillers, retakes and silences.
- **Paraphrase** has one button. A language model reads the transcript and proposes cutting filled
  pauses, false starts, hedges and repetition, including the same point made twice in different
  words, and can move words where a sentence reads better. There is no word list, and it has been
  tested on English speech. See [AI cleanup](/docs/ai-cleanup).

We have not run the same recording through all three, so this compares approaches, not results.

**Generated speech.** Riverside's AI Voice "simulates your speaking voice" from text, and VideoDub
regenerates up to two words with lip sync. Async offers over 1,000 AI voices and a clone that makes
"new speech you never actually recorded". Paraphrase **never generates speech**, so a word you never
said cannot appear in an export. See [why Paraphrase never generates
speech](/blog/why-paraphrase-never-generates-speech).

## Pricing

Checked on 17 October 2026. We have not converted currencies.

**Riverside** showed us, as a UK visitor, pounds: Pro £35 a month or £24 a month billed yearly (£288
a year), Grow £45 or £34 (£408 a year), Webinar £99 or £79 (£948 a year), Business on request. Pro,
Grow and Webinar offer a 14-day free trial, which its help centre says needs a card. Recording is
unlimited, but separate track downloads are capped at 15 hours a month on Pro, 20 on Grow and 25 on
Webinar. Riverside also has AI credits for features such as voice clone audio and AI B-roll, with
2,000 to start on Pro. See [Riverside pricing](/blog/riverside-pricing).

**Podcastle (Async)** showed us pounds: Essentials £19.99 a month or £11.99 on yearly billing, Pro
£39.99 or £23.99, Teams £84.99 or £49.99 per creator. Remote recording is 20 hours on Pro and 50 on
Teams; for Essentials the plan card says 4 hours and the comparison table 2. Trial length is not
stated. See [Podcastle pricing](/blog/podcastle-pricing).

**Paraphrase** Premium is £13.99 a month, £83.85 a year or £179.65 for three years (about £4.99 a
month), with 120 hours of transcription a month. Credits are spent only at transcription, so
re-editing and re-exporting are free, and cleanup uses no credits within its daily limit (3 a day
free, 30 on Premium). See [pricing](/pricing).

## What Paraphrase does not do

Against two studios, this is most of the list:

- No remote recording, guests, multitrack or separate per-speaker tracks.
- No noise removal, enhancement, levelling, EQ, music or sound effects.
- No silence remover, speaker labels, captions, subtitles or translation.
- No hosting, publishing, live streaming, collaboration, comments or share links.
- No AI voice, cloning, overdub or text to speech.

## Which should you pick?

**You interview guests and also stream or run webinars:** Riverside. Our
[Riverside review](/blog/riverside-review) covers it.

**You interview guests and want AI voices and hosting with an RSS feed:** Podcastle. See our
[Podcastle review](/blog/podcastle-review).

**You record in a language outside Podcastle's five:** Riverside, or Paraphrase for its 25 European
languages.

**You record solo (narration, a course, a one-person show):** Paraphrase. See
[podcast editing](/podcast-editing) and [course editing](/course-editing).

**You record guests in a studio but cut your own segments separately:** keep the studio and use
Paraphrase for those segments. It takes uploads up to 4 hours per recording and exports WAV or FLAC.
The pairwise posts are [Paraphrase vs Riverside](/blog/paraphrase-vs-riverside) and
[Paraphrase vs Podcastle](/blog/paraphrase-vs-podcastle).
