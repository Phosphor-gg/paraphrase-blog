# Descript alternatives: the honest options for editing by transcript

**Bottom line:** none of the alternatives below is a like-for-like replacement for Descript, which
combines transcript editing, video, remote recording, screen recording, captions, audio clean-up
and AI voices in one product. If you want the remote studio, look at **Riverside**. If noise and
echo are the problem, look at **Adobe Podcast**. If you only want to cut your own spoken audio by
its transcript, with an AI cleanup you review and no generated speech, **Paraphrase** does that one
job and nothing else. If you use most of what Descript does, stay with Descript.

Descript's own feature page puts its core idea plainly: "Text-based editing lets you edit video and audio by editing the transcript."
Around that it has built a full production suite. People looking for alternatives are usually
after one of a few things, and switching only solves some of them.

All Descript details below come from Descript's own pricing, features and Rooms pages and its
help centre, read on 17 October 2026.

## Why people look for Descript alternatives

**It does far more than they use.** Descript lists Underlord (an AI co-editor), captions, screen
recording, Eye Contact, Green Screen, clips, translation and dubbing, Studio Sound, Rooms for
remote recording, and more. If all you do is tidy a voice recording, you are paying for a large
toolbox.

**Allowances are measured two ways.** Paid plans come with media hours and AI credits each month.
Media hours count "uploaded media files and media recorded in Descript, regardless of whether
they're transcribed", AI credits are spent by features such as Studio Sound and Remove filler words,
and Descript's help centre says unused amounts "don't roll over into the next cycle". We have
covered [what Descript costs](/blog/descript-pricing) in detail.

**Some people do not want generated speech anywhere near their voice.** Descript's Regenerate and
voice clones can produce words you never recorded. Descript gates this carefully (a clone needs
"explicit recorded authorization" from the speaker), but some podcasters, journalists and
narrators would rather use a tool where that cannot happen at all.

**Platform.** Descript's desktop app needs macOS 14 or Windows 11 or later, and the web version
officially supports Chromium-based browsers. Descript says it is not officially supported on
mobile browsers or tablets.

## Alternatives that replace part of Descript

### Riverside

**The closest match if remote recording is why you use Descript.** Riverside's pricing page offers
a free plan with "2 hours of multi-track recordings (Separate audio and video tracks)", and its Pro
plan lists "Unlimited text-based editing" and removing "silences & filler words". Its help centre
lists text-based editing on all plans, and AI Voice, which generates speech in your own
authenticated voice, on paid plans. Descript's own equivalent is Rooms, which records every
participant locally "in up to 4K" with a separate file each, so the two are close in shape.

**Best for:** shows built around remote guests, where recording quality matters more than editing.

### Adobe Podcast

Two tools that run in the browser. **Enhance Speech** removes "noise and echo from voice
recordings", and **Studio**, labelled beta, records, transcribes and edits: you "simply delete text
in the transcript to remove the corresponding audio or video from your project". Studio also
records remote guests, with speaker-separated downloads of the originals on the paid plan. There
is a free plan with daily limits; the paid route for new customers is now Adobe's Firefly Pro
plan, shown at $19.99 a month on podcast.adobe.com on 17 October 2026. Filler words appear in the
transcript and you delete the ones you want gone yourself.

**Best for:** making a rough recording sound cleaner, with light transcript editing alongside.

### Audacity

Free and open source, and its site says it will "always remain free for everyone". It runs on
Windows, macOS and Linux. It is a waveform editor, so you cut by listening and selecting audio,
not by reading words.

**Best for:** anyone who wants full manual control and pays nothing.

## What you will struggle to replace

Be straight with yourself about this before switching. **Descript's breadth is the point of
Descript.** Transcript editing for video as well as audio, multitrack transcription with speaker
detection, Studio Sound for noise and echo, screen recording, captions, translation and dubbing,
Rooms, and editor comments for working with others. Its Edit for Clarity tool is also a genuine
one-pass tidy-up: it "analyzes your script to clean up pacing, tighten delivery, and remove
unnecessary or off-topic content", at an intensity you choose, and keeps the cuts as ignored text
you can restore.

No single alternative in the table matches all of that. Swapping Descript for one of them usually
means swapping one bundle for two or three tools.

## If you only edit your own voice

**Paraphrase** is what we make, so weigh this accordingly. It does one thing: you record or upload,
every word is transcribed with a timestamp, and you edit by choosing which of your words stay and
in what order. The export is your original recording with pieces cut out, joined at word
boundaries.

Where it differs from Descript:

- **It never generates speech.** No voice cloning, no overdub, no text to speech. A word you never
  said cannot appear in an export because there is no audio for it.
- **Cleanup is one button and a proposal.** A language model reads the transcript and proposes cuts
  for filled pauses, false starts, hedges and repetition (including the same point made twice in
  different words), and can move words where a sentence reads better. Every cut shows struck
  through and any word can be put back. It has been tested on English speech. See
  [how AI cleanup works](/docs/ai-cleanup).
- **Credits are spent once, at transcription.** One credit transcribes one second of audio.
  Editing, cleanup and export use no credits, so re-editing and re-exporting are free. Cleanup has
  a daily limit instead (30 a day on Premium, 3 on the free plan).
- **Lossless and compressed exports.** WAV and FLAC (lossless), MP3, M4A and Opus, plus MP4 when
  the project has a video take.

Where it does not compete, plainly: no noise removal or enhancement, no remote recording studio or
multitrack, no speaker labels, captions or translation, no collaboration, no screen recording, and
no desktop app. Premium is £13.99 a month, or about £4.99 a month on the three year cycle, with
120 hours of audio a month. The [full comparison](/blog/paraphrase-vs-descript) goes through it
side by side.

**Best for:** [podcast](/podcast-editing), [voiceover](/voiceover-editing) and
[interview](/interview-editing) recordings where you want clean cuts of your real voice and
nothing else.

## Do you actually need to replace Descript?

Often not. If you use its video editing, Studio Sound, captions or Rooms, every alternative here
gives you less, and our [Descript review](/blog/descript-review) is fairly enthusiastic about what
it does well.

Switching makes sense if you use a small part of it and the allowance model does not suit how you
work, or if you specifically want a tool that cannot generate speech. Running Paraphrase for the
spoken edit and something else for the rest is also a reasonable setup.

The quickest test is your own recording. [Make a free account](https://paraphrase.bot), no card
needed, and you get 15 minutes of transcription to see whether editing by transcript, without
generated speech, is enough for what you make.
