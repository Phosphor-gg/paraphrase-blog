# Descript vs Podcastle vs Paraphrase: which editor for your spoken audio?

**Bottom line:** Descript and Podcastle (now Async) are both full studios: recording, a transcript
editor, sound clean-up and AI voices. Pick **Podcastle** if you want remote recording, AI voices and
podcast hosting with an RSS feed in one browser tab. Pick **Descript** if you work in more
languages than Podcastle lists, want desktop apps and screen recording, or want Edit for Clarity to
tighten a script. Pick **Paraphrase** if the job is cutting your own voice: one AI cleanup you
review word by word, words you can drag into a new order, credits spent only at transcription, and
no generated speech, ever.

## What each one is for

**Descript** is an audio and video editor you drive by its transcript. It lists captions, screen
recording, Studio Sound, Remove Filler Words, Remove Retakes, Edit for Clarity, Regenerate,
translation and dubbing, and Rooms for remote recording. It has macOS and Windows apps and a web
version for Chromium browsers.

**Podcastle** has become Async: podcastle.ai redirects to async.com, whose podcast page is headed
"Formerly Podcastle". The podcast studio records remote guests on separate tracks in up to 4K video
and WAV audio, edits on a multitrack timeline or in Text Mode, cleans up sound with Magic Dust,
generates voices, and hosts the show. Async as a whole describes itself as a "Chat-based AI creative
suite for video content production". It runs in the browser, with an iPhone app for recording.

**Paraphrase** is a browser editor for your own recording. Every word gets a timestamp, and every
edit is a choice of which of your words stay and in what order. The export is your original
recording with pieces cut out at word boundaries.

## Where they overlap

All three let you remove a word by removing it from the transcript. Podcastle's Text Mode will
"Remove a word or group of words from both the transcript and the audio file", with a warning that
correcting a word changes the transcript only. Descript's Edit for Clarity keeps its cuts as ignored
text you can restore. Paraphrase leaves every cut word on screen, struck through, and one click restores
it.

The automatic cleanup is where they differ.

- **Descript's** Remove filler words lists each detected filler with a timestamp and preview, and
  "currently detects filler words in English transcripts only". Edit for Clarity goes further,
  cutting "unnecessary or off-topic content" at Low, Medium or Heavy intensity. Both use AI credits.
- **Podcastle's** Filler tab finds a preset list ("mmm, um, uh, uhm, uhh, umm, mm, mhm") to remove
  one by one or all at once, and its Silences tab finds gaps longer than half a second by default.
  Rough Cut, for video, removes filler words, retakes and silences.
- **Paraphrase** has one button. A language model reads the transcript and proposes cutting filled
  pauses, false starts, hedges and repetition, including the same point made twice in different
  words, and can move words where a sentence reads better. There is no word list, and the result is
  a proposal you review. It has been tested on English speech. See
  [AI cleanup](/docs/ai-cleanup).

We have not run the same recording through all three, so this compares approaches, not results.

**Generated speech** separates Paraphrase from both. Descript's Regenerate types new words into an
authorised custom voice, and Edit for Clarity's "Reword for flow" can regenerate audio with AI speech. Async offers
"1000+ AI voices" and a voice clone that creates, in its own words, "new speech you never actually
recorded". Paraphrase **never generates speech**, so a word you never said cannot appear in an
export. If generated intros or sponsor reads are part of your show, that is a real strength of the
other two.

limit (3 a day free, 30 on Premium). See [pricing](/pricing).

## What Paraphrase does not do

Descript and Podcastle both cover ground Paraphrase does not touch:

- No remote recording studio, multitrack or separate per-speaker tracks.
- No noise removal, enhancement, levelling, EQ, music or sound effects.
- No silence remover; the cleanup cuts words, not gaps.
- No speaker labels, captions, subtitles or translation.
- No hosting, collaboration, comments or share links.
- No AI voice, cloning, overdub or text to speech.

## Which should you pick?

**You host a show with remote guests and want one tool from recording to RSS:** Podcastle. Our
[Podcastle review](/blog/podcastle-review) covers it in depth.

**You make video or tutorials, or record in a language Podcastle does not list:** Descript, with
screen recording and around 25 transcription languages. See our
[Descript review](/blog/descript-review).

**You record yourself and most of your editing time goes on stumbles and repetition:** Paraphrase.
It suits a solo [podcast](/podcast-editing), [voiceover](/voiceover-editing) or
[course](/course-editing).

**You need certainty that every word in the export was spoken:** Paraphrase, because it has no way
to generate one.

**You like your current studio:** keep it, and use Paraphrase for your own narration if you want,
then bring the WAV or FLAC back. The pairwise posts are
[Paraphrase vs Descript](/blog/paraphrase-vs-descript) and
[Paraphrase vs Podcastle](/blog/paraphrase-vs-podcastle).
