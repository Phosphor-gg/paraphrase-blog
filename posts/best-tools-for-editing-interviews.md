# The best tools for editing recorded interviews

**Bottom line:** if you are still to record the interview and the guest is remote, use
**Riverside**: each person is recorded locally on a separate track, the transcript is labelled by
speaker, and you edit by text in the same place. **Descript** is the stronger choice if the
interview is also a video you will produce heavily. If the edit has to stand up as what the
interviewee actually said (journalism, research, oral history, testimonials), **Paraphrase** (ours)
is built so that it cannot generate speech at all, and every cut stays visible in the transcript.
It has no speaker labels or separate tracks, so it suits interviews you already have as one
recording. **Adobe Podcast** records guests on separate tracks and has strong noise clean-up in
Enhance Speech. **Cleanvoice** is a clean-up pass that handles up to five tracks in sync, and
**Audacity** the free manual option.

Interviews have problems a solo recording does not: two or more voices, crosstalk, a guest on a
bad connection, and an obligation to the person you interviewed. That last one is easy to forget
when choosing software.

## The question specific to interviews: can it put words in someone's mouth?

When you edit your own voice, generated speech is a convenience. When you edit someone else's, it
is a question of trust. Four of the tools here can generate speech, and three document a consent
step: Descript requires "explicit recorded authorization" from the person whose voice is cloned and
refuses non-consenting people; Riverside's VideoDub requires a legal disclaimer before it is used on
a guest's track, and a guest voice clone (Business plan) needs the guest to record a consent video;
Async uses a "Voice Captcha" for cloning. CapCut's voice clone overdub page says only that it is
"only available in certain regions". Adobe Podcast's own pages describe no speech generation, but
Firefly Pro, the plan new customers now buy for Podcast Premium, includes Firefly's Generate Speech.

**Paraphrase leads on this axis**, and it is ours, so weigh that. It never generates speech: no
cloning, no overdub, no text to speech. The export is the original recording with pieces cut out at
word boundaries, so a word the interviewee never said cannot appear in it. Every cut stays on
screen struck through, so you can always see what was removed. If you need to be able to say "every
word in this edit was spoken by this person", that is a guarantee of the tool rather than a setting.
We explain the reasoning in [why Paraphrase never generates speech](/blog/why-paraphrase-never-generates-speech).

## The full list, ordered by how much of the interview job each covers

That job is recording, separating speakers, cutting by transcript and cleaning the sound. On that
axis, Paraphrase comes fifth.

## 1. Riverside: recording and editing remote interviews in one place

Riverside records "locally on each participant's computer or device", so a guest's poor connection
should not ruin their track, and each person gets their own audio and video. The transcript labels
"each speaker with the display name they use in the Studio". When you cut text where speakers
overlap, it asks which speaker to remove, which is exactly the problem interviews create. It also
removes crosstalk and background noise, and transcribes roughly 100 languages.

You can upload an interview recorded elsewhere and choose the number of speakers, up to 4 hours per
file. The free plan offers "Try 2 hours" of multitrack recording, with a Riverside watermark. Pro is
£35 a month, or £24 a month billed yearly (£288), as shown to a UK visitor.

**Best for:** remote interviews recorded and edited in one place. See our
[Riverside review](/blog/riverside-review).

## 2. Descript: for heavily produced video interviews

Descript's Rooms records each participant locally "in up to 4K" with a separate file each, and its
editor offers multitrack transcription with speaker detection, text-based editing of audio and
video, Studio Sound, captions, and editor comments for working with a producer. Transcription
covers around 25 languages, with one language per file.

**Best for:** video interviews with captions and a team. See our
[Descript review](/blog/descript-review).

## 3. Podcastle (now Async): recording plus hosting

Async records "separate audio and video tracks for every guest locally", with up to 10 participants
and an iPhone app for remote interviews. Its transcript shows "separate lines for each recording
participant", Text Mode edits the multitrack audio from it, and you can export the transcript as a
PDF or Doc. Transcription is listed in five languages.

**Best for:** interview podcasts that also need hosting. See our [Podcastle review](/blog/podcastle-review).

## 4. Adobe Podcast: separate tracks and Enhance Speech

Adobe Podcast Studio (labelled beta) records remote guests from a shared link; each participant's
audio "is captured directly in their browser and continuously saved", giving "separate,
high-quality tracks for each speaker". You can also upload multitrack audio or video recorded
elsewhere, including from Zoom, Restream, StreamYard or Riverside, and it syncs them "with a single
unified transcript". Select "Detect speakers" when transcribing to label speakers, though Adobe says
some users will not see the option "depending on their plan or region". You edit by deleting text
in the transcript.

Enhance Speech is the strength: it removes noise and echo, which matters when a guest recorded in a
bad room. One limit for interviews: in Studio "you can only enhance at the project level", not per
speaker. Downloading the speaker-separated original recordings needs Firefly Pro, Studio runs only in
desktop Chrome, and transcription covers seven languages. See our
[Adobe Podcast review](/blog/adobe-podcast-review).

**Best for:** remote interviews where the guest's sound needs cleaning up.

## 5. Paraphrase: when the words have to be theirs

Beyond never generating speech, Paraphrase handles the practical side of a recorded interview
simply. Upload the file (audio or video, most formats), or paste a link, up to 4 hours per
recording. Every word is transcribed with a timestamp in 25 European languages. Click words to cut,
drag to reorder, and the AI cleanup proposes trims for false starts, hedges and repetition, any of
which you can reverse word by word. Several takes in a project edit as one transcript, and an MP4 export cuts the
picture at the same words. Re-editing is free after transcription.

What it lacks for interviews, plainly: no speaker labels, so you tell speakers apart by reading; no
separate tracks; no remote recording; no noise removal. More on
[editing interviews with Paraphrase](/interview-editing).

**Best for:** journalists, researchers and anyone editing an existing interview recording who
needs a verifiable cut.

## 6. Cleanvoice: clean-up for multitrack files

Cleanvoice edits "up to 5 tracks" together, keeping them in sync, and removes fillers, stutters,
mouth sounds, breaths, dead air and background noise. It can mute instead of cutting so video
stays aligned. Changes to individual edits go through a marker export to another editor, and each
processing run is billed, so reprocessing costs again.

**Best for:** cleaning each speaker's track before or after the main edit. See our
[Cleanvoice review](/blog/cleanvoice-review).

## 7. CapCut: clips from video interviews

CapCut's transcript-based editing and auto captions suit cutting a filmed interview into short
social clips. Its pages do not describe multi-speaker audio editing. See our
[CapCut review](/blog/capcut-review).

## 8. Audacity: free multitrack, by hand

Audacity edits multiple tracks on the waveform for free, with built-in Noise Reduction and a
compressor (the Audacity 3 manual also documents loudness normalisation). There is no transcript
editing, so you find each quote by ear.

**Best for:** short interviews and no budget. See our [Audacity review](/blog/audacity-review).

Paraphrase is ours. It leads on never generating speech and on showing every cut; it does not lead
on recording, speaker separation or sound, which is why it is fifth on the main list. We have no
customer reviews to cite.

## How to choose an interview editor

- **Not recorded yet, guest remote:** Riverside, Descript Rooms, Async or Adobe Podcast Studio.
  Separate tracks save you later.
- **Lots of crosstalk:** Riverside's overlap handling, or separate tracks cleaned in Cleanvoice.
- **Published as someone's own words:** Paraphrase, or any tool with its speech features left off
  and a policy that they stay off.
- **Language other than the big European ones:** Riverside lists around 100 transcription
  languages, and CapCut's transcript page claims 100+.
- **No budget:** Audacity.
