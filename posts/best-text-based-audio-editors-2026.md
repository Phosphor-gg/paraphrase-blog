# The best text-based audio and video editors in 2026

**Bottom line:** on this list's axis, how much of a typical creator's editing a tool covers by
transcript, **Descript** comes first, because it edits both audio and video by transcript and wraps
a full production suite around it. If you record guests remotely, **Riverside** is built around a
remote recording studio, with transcript editing on top. **Adobe Podcast** pairs a transcript
editor (Studio, in beta) with Enhance Speech, its noise and echo clean-up. If the job is cutting
your own spoken recording word by word, with nothing generated and re-edits that use no credits,
that is what **Paraphrase** (ours) does and all it does. **Audacity** is free and edits on the
waveform rather than the transcript, so it sits at the end as the free, non-text option.

A text-based editor transcribes your recording with a timestamp on every word, so deleting a word
in the text deletes it from the audio or video, instead of hunting through a waveform for the
right half-second.

## 1. Descript: a full production suite around the transcript

Descript's own feature page says it plainly: "Text-based editing lets you edit video and audio by
editing the transcript." It adds that "moving a sentence moves the footage with it". Around that
it has built nearly everything else: captions, screen recording, Studio Sound for noise and echo,
multitrack transcription with speaker detection, translation and dubbing, and Rooms, a
browser-based remote recording studio where every participant gets a separate file.

It also offers AI speech: text to speech, stock AI speakers, Regenerate and custom voice clones
can produce words you never recorded. A custom voice clone needs recorded consent from the person
whose voice it is.

Where it runs out: allowances come as monthly media hours and AI credits that do not roll over,
Studio Sound and Remove Filler Words cost up to 30 AI credits per file each time you apply them,
filler-word detection is English only, and the desktop app needs macOS 14 or Windows 11 or later.
Our [Descript review](/blog/descript-review) and [pricing breakdown](/blog/descript-pricing) go
deeper.

**Best for:** people who want one tool for editing audio and video, captions and cleanup, and will
use most of it.

## 2. Riverside: built around recording guests remotely

Riverside is a recording studio first. Its pages describe tracks "recorded locally on each
participant's computer or device", in up to 4K on paid plans, with separate audio and video for
each person. Its help centre lists text-based editing on "All plans": click or drag across words,
press Delete, and removed text stays "shown in gray with a strikethrough". You can also mute words
rather than cut them, and move or duplicate media by copying and pasting text.

For interviews it has one detail worth knowing: when speakers overlap, it asks which speaker to
remove. Transcripts are labelled with each person's display name from the studio. It also offers AI
Voice, voice cloning (in beta) and VideoDub on paid plans.

Where it runs out: the free plan offers "Try 2 hours" of multitrack recording, up to 720p, with a
Riverside watermark, and paid plans cap separate-track downloads (15 hours a month on Pro). Pro is
£35 a month, or £24 a month billed yearly (£288), as shown to a UK visitor. See our
[Riverside review](/blog/riverside-review).

**Best for:** remote interviews and multi-guest shows where recording quality matters most.

## 3. Adobe Podcast: transcript editing with Enhance Speech beside it

Adobe Podcast has two tools: Enhance Speech, to "Remove noise and echo from voice recordings", and
Studio, labelled beta, where Adobe says you "cut, copy, and paste your audio just like a text
document". Its FAQ: "simply delete text in the transcript to remove the corresponding audio or
video from your project." Studio takes multitrack audio and video uploads with each track kept
separate, records remote guests by link on separate tracks, can detect speakers, and adds captions,
a music library and audiograms.

Enhance Speech is a genuine strength, and one Paraphrase does not have at all: Adobe says it "makes
voice recordings sound as if they were recorded in a professional podcasting studio", and the paid
plan adds sliders for speech, background noise and music. Its FAQ says Enhance Speech "does not
generate or translate". Since 30 August 2026, new customers get Podcast Premium through Adobe
Firefly Pro, and that plan includes Firefly's Generate Speech.

Where it runs out: Studio "is only supported on Google Chrome (desktop)", transcription covers seven
languages, and Adobe's own editing guides have you pick out filler words in the transcript and
delete them yourself. On the free plan, Studio downloads are limited to two projects a day of up to
30 minutes each. See our [Adobe Podcast review](/blog/adobe-podcast-review),
[Adobe Podcast pricing](/blog/adobe-podcast-pricing) and
[Paraphrase vs Adobe Podcast](/blog/paraphrase-vs-adobe-podcast).

**Best for:** spoken recordings that need their sound cleaned up as much as their words cut, if you
work in desktop Chrome.

## 4. Paraphrase: cutting your own words, nothing generated

Paraphrase is ours, so weigh this section accordingly. It does one job. You record in the browser,
upload a file, or paste a link, and every word is transcribed with a timestamp. Click a word to cut
it and it stays on screen struck through, so you can click it again to put it back. Drag a word to
move it. The export is your original recording with pieces cut out, joined at word boundaries, as
WAV, FLAC, MP3, M4A or Opus, or MP4 when there is a video take.

Where it genuinely leads:

- **It never generates speech.** No voice cloning, no overdub, no text to speech. A word you never
  said cannot appear in an export because there is no audio for it.
  [Why we built it that way](/blog/why-paraphrase-never-generates-speech).
- **Every cut is reviewable, word by word.** The one-button [AI cleanup](/docs/ai-cleanup) proposes
  cuts for filled pauses, false starts, hedges and repetition, and every one shows struck through
  until you decide.
- **Re-editing is free.** Credits are spent once, at transcription. Editing, cleanup and export
  use no credits; cleanup has a daily limit of 3 runs on the free plan and 30 on Premium.

Where it does not compete: no noise removal or enhancement, no remote recording, no multitrack or
speaker labels, no captions, no collaboration, and no desktop app. Transcription covers 25 European
languages, and cleanup has been tested on English.

**Best for:** solo podcasts, voiceovers, narration and talking-head video where you want clean
cuts of your real voice and nothing else.

## 5. Audacity: free, and edited on the waveform

Audacity is free and open source (GPL v3) and runs on Windows, macOS and Linux. You edit on the
waveform: select a section by clicking and dragging, then press Delete. Its built-in Noise
Reduction removes steady background noise such as hiss, hum and fan noise. It pairs well with a
text-based editor: clean the sound in Audacity, cut the words elsewhere. See our
[Audacity review](/blog/audacity-review) and
[Audacity vs Descript vs Paraphrase](/blog/audacity-vs-descript-vs-paraphrase).

**Best for:** anyone who wants a free editor and is happy to work on the waveform.

## How to choose

Start with what happens before and after the edit, not the edit itself.

- **Do you record other people remotely?** Then the recording studio matters most. Riverside, or
  Descript's Rooms, or Adobe Podcast Studio.
- **Is the output mostly video with captions and effects?** Descript covers that best here.
- **Need noise removal?** Every tool here except Paraphrase has some. Adobe Podcast's Enhance
  Speech has a free plan (up to 1 hour a day, files up to 30 minutes), and Audacity's Noise
  Reduction is free.
- **Want generated speech impossible, not just switched off?** That is Paraphrase's defining choice.
- **Not English?** Check language lists. Descript's filler detection is English only and Adobe
  Podcast transcribes seven languages.
