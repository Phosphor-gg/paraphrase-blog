# The best text-based audio and video editors in 2026

**Bottom line:** for most people the best text-based editor is **Descript**, because it edits
both audio and video by transcript and wraps a full production suite around it. If you record
guests remotely, **Riverside** is the better pick, since the recording studio is the core of the
product and transcript editing is built on top. For short social video, **CapCut** has transcript
editing inside a full video editor. **Podcastle (now Async)** suits people who want recording,
transcript editing and podcast hosting in one place. If the job is cutting your own spoken
recording word by word, with nothing generated and re-edits that cost nothing, that is what
**Paraphrase** (ours) does and all it does.

A text-based editor transcribes your recording with a timestamp on every word, so deleting a word
in the text deletes it from the audio or video. It is much faster than hunting through a waveform
for the right half-second.

## 1. Descript: the most complete text-based editor

Descript's own feature page says it plainly: "Text-based editing lets you edit video and audio by
editing the transcript." Its home page adds that you can "cut, trim, and rearrange the video by
changing the transcript". Around that it has built nearly everything else: captions, screen
recording, Studio Sound for noise and echo, multitrack transcription with speaker detection,
translation and dubbing, and Rooms, a browser-based remote recording studio where every
participant gets a separate file.

It also offers opt-in AI speech: Regenerate and custom voice clones can produce words you never
recorded, behind a recorded consent step.

Where it runs out: allowances come as monthly media hours and AI credits that do not roll over,
filler-word detection is English only, and the desktop app needs macOS 14 or Windows 11 or later.
Our [Descript review](/blog/descript-review) and [pricing breakdown](/blog/descript-pricing) go
deeper.

**Best for:** people who want one tool for editing audio and video, captions and cleanup, and will
use most of it.

## 2. Riverside: best if you record guests remotely

Riverside is a recording studio first. Its pages describe tracks "recorded locally on each
participant's computer or device", in up to 4K with separate audio and video for each person. Its
help centre lists text-based editing on "All plans": click or drag across words, press Delete, and
removed text stays "shown in gray with a strikethrough". You can also mute words rather than cut
them, and move or duplicate media by copying and pasting text.

For interviews it has one detail worth knowing: when speakers overlap, it asks which speaker to
remove. Transcripts are labelled with each person's display name from the studio. It also offers AI
Voice, voice cloning (in beta) and VideoDub on paid plans, and transcribes roughly 100 languages.

Where it runs out: the free plan gives two hours of multitrack recording, once, with a watermark. See our [Riverside review](/blog/riverside-review).

**Best for:** remote interviews and multi-guest shows where recording quality matters most.

## 3. CapCut: transcript editing inside a full video editor

CapCut is a video editor for desktop, mobile and the browser, and "Transcript-based editing" is one
of its tools: deleting transcript text removes the matching video, and corrections carry into the
caption track. It highlights filler words and gaps, and its own guidance is sensible: "Treat
highlighting as a shortlist, not a verdict."

It also has templates, effects, auto captions, text to speech, and an AI voice clone overdub
"only available in certain regions".

Where it runs out: its pages disagree on how many transcription languages it supports, and they do
not make clear whether filler-word removal needs Pro, so check in the app. Premium elements can add
a watermark at export. More in our [CapCut review](/blog/capcut-review).

**Best for:** short-form and social video where captions, effects and templates matter as much as
the cut.

## 4. Podcastle (now Async): recording, editing and hosting together

Podcastle now redirects to Async, whose podcast page is headed "Formerly Podcastle". Its Text Mode
"lets you edit your multi-track audio files directly from a transcript". One thing to know: clicking
a word gives both "Correct", which changes only the transcript, and "Remove", which cuts the word
from the audio. Filler detection, silence removal, Magic Dust noise clean-up, remote recording and
podcast hosting sit alongside, as do more than 1,000 AI voices and voice cloning.

Where it runs out: its help centre lists transcription in five languages (English, Spanish, German,
French and Italian), and we could not confirm which plans include text-based editing. Our
[Podcastle review](/blog/podcastle-review) covers it.

**Best for:** podcasters who want to record, edit, clean and host without stitching tools together.

## Not text-based, but often on the same shortlist

### Cleanvoice

Cleanvoice's own FAQ says it "doesn't work based on the transcript-to-edit flow". It removes filler
words, stutters, mouth sounds, breaths, silences and noise automatically, so it pairs with a
text-based editor rather than replacing one. See our [Cleanvoice review](/blog/cleanvoice-review).

### Audacity

Audacity is free and open source, and you edit on the waveform. An optional Whisper plugin
transcribes into a label track, but its page does not describe editing audio through that
transcript, and its download page says the plugins are not yet compatible with the latest version. See our [Audacity review](/blog/audacity-review).

## How to choose

Start with what happens before and after the edit, not the edit itself.

- **Do you record other people remotely?** Then the recording studio matters most. Riverside, or
  Descript's Rooms, or Podcastle (now Async).
- **Is the output mostly video with captions and effects?** CapCut for social clips, Descript for
  longer pieces.
- **Need noise removal?** Every tool here except Paraphrase has some; Cleanvoice specialises in it.
- **Want generated speech impossible, not just switched off?** That is Paraphrase's defining choice.
- **Not English?** Check language lists. Descript's filler detection is English only, and Podcastle
  lists five transcription languages.
