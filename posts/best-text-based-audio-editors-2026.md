# The best text-based audio and video editors in 2026

**Bottom line:** on this list's axis, how much of a typical creator's editing a tool covers by
transcript, **Descript** comes first, because it edits both audio and video by transcript and wraps
a full production suite around it. If you record guests remotely, **Riverside** is built around a
remote recording studio, with transcript editing on top. For short social video, **CapCut** has
transcript editing inside a full video editor. **Podcastle (now Async)** puts recording, transcript
editing and podcast hosting in one place. **Adobe Podcast** pairs a transcript editor (Studio, in
beta) with Enhance Speech, its noise and echo clean-up. If the job is cutting your own spoken
recording word by word, with nothing generated and re-edits that use no credits, that is what
**Paraphrase** (ours) does and all it does.

A text-based editor transcribes your recording with a timestamp on every word, so deleting a word
in the text deletes it from the audio or video, instead of hunting through a waveform for the
right half-second.

## 1. Descript: a full production suite around the transcript

Descript's own feature page says it plainly: "Text-based editing lets you edit video and audio by
editing the transcript." It adds that "moving a sentence moves the footage with it". Around that
it has built nearly everything else: captions, screen
recording, Studio Sound for noise and echo, multitrack transcription with speaker detection,
translation and dubbing, and Rooms, a browser-based remote recording studio where every
participant gets a separate file.

It also offers AI speech: text to speech, stock AI speakers, Regenerate and custom voice clones
can produce words you never recorded. A custom voice clone needs recorded consent from the person
whose voice it is.

Where it runs out: allowances come as monthly media hours and AI credits that do not roll over,
Studio Sound and Remove Filler Words cost up to 30 AI credits per file each time you apply them,
filler-word detection is English only, and the desktop app needs macOS 14 or Windows 11 or later.
Our [Descript review](/blog/descript-review) and [pricing breakdown](/blog/descript-pricing) go
deeper.

**Best for:** people who want one tool for editing audio and video, captions and cleanup, and will
use most of it.

## 2. Riverside: built around recording guests remotely

Riverside is a recording studio first. Its pages describe tracks "recorded locally on each
participant's computer or device", in up to 4K on paid plans, with separate audio and video for
each person. Its
help centre lists text-based editing on "All plans": click or drag across words, press Delete, and
removed text stays "shown in gray with a strikethrough". You can also mute words rather than cut
them, and move or duplicate media by copying and pasting text.

For interviews it has one detail worth knowing: when speakers overlap, it asks which speaker to
remove. Transcripts are labelled with each person's display name from the studio. It also offers AI
Voice, voice cloning (in beta) and VideoDub on paid plans, and transcribes roughly 100 languages.

Where it runs out: the free plan offers "Try 2 hours" of multitrack recording, up to 720p, with a
Riverside watermark, and paid plans cap separate-track downloads (15 hours a month on Pro). Pro is
£35 a month, or £24 a month billed yearly (£288), as shown to a UK visitor. See our
[Riverside review](/blog/riverside-review).

**Best for:** remote interviews and multi-guest shows where recording quality matters most.

## 3. CapCut: transcript editing inside a full video editor

CapCut is a video editor for desktop, mobile and the browser, and "Transcript-based editing" is one
of its tools: deleting transcript text removes the matching video, and corrections carry into the
caption track. It recognises filler words and speech gaps in the transcript and, per its own
pages, can remove filler words in one click. A CapCut guide adds sensible advice: "Treat
highlighting as a shortlist, not a verdict."

It also has templates, effects, auto captions, text to speech, and an AI voice clone overdub
"only available in certain regions".

Where it runs out: its transcript page claims 100+ languages, which we did not check in the app,
and its pages do not make clear whether filler-word removal needs Pro, so check in the app. Premium elements can add
a watermark at export. More in our [CapCut review](/blog/capcut-review).

**Best for:** short-form and social video where captions, effects and templates matter as much as
the cut.

## 4. Podcastle (now Async): recording, editing and hosting together

Podcastle now redirects to Async, whose podcast page is headed "Formerly Podcastle". Its Text Mode
"lets you edit your multi-track audio files directly from a transcript". One thing to know: clicking
a word gives both "Correct", which changes only the transcript, and "Remove", which cuts the word
from the audio. A Filler tab can remove its preset fillers one by one or all at once, and a
Silences tab finds gaps longer than half a second by default. Magic Dust noise clean-up (paid
plans), remote recording and podcast hosting sit alongside, as do more than 1,000 AI voices and
voice cloning (Pro and above).

Where it runs out: its help centre lists transcription in five languages (English, Spanish, German,
French and Italian), and its pricing table marks text-based editing as "Limited" on the free plan
without saying what the limit is. Our [Podcastle review](/blog/podcastle-review) covers it.

**Best for:** podcasters who want to record, edit, clean and host without stitching tools together.

## 5. Adobe Podcast: transcript editing with Enhance Speech beside it

Adobe Podcast has two tools: Enhance Speech, to "Remove noise and echo from voice recordings", and
Studio, labelled beta, where Adobe says you "cut, copy, and paste your audio just like a text
document". Its FAQ: "simply delete text in the transcript to remove the corresponding audio or
video from your project." Studio takes multitrack audio and video uploads with each track kept
separate, records remote guests by link on separate tracks, can detect speakers (Adobe says the
option depends on plan or region), and adds captions, a music library and audiograms.

Enhance Speech is a genuine strength, and one Paraphrase does not have at all: Adobe says it "makes
voice recordings sound as if they were recorded in a professional podcasting studio", and the paid
plan adds sliders for speech, background noise and music. Adobe Podcast's own pages describe no AI
voice or text to speech, and its FAQ says Enhance Speech "does not generate or translate". Since 30
August 2026, though, new customers get Podcast Premium through Adobe Firefly Pro, and that plan
includes Firefly's Generate Speech.

Where it runs out: Studio "is only supported on Google Chrome (desktop)", transcription covers seven
languages, and its pages describe finding filler words in the transcript and deleting them yourself,
with no automatic filler or pause removal. On the free plan, Studio downloads are limited to two
projects a day of up to 30 minutes each. See our [Adobe Podcast review](/blog/adobe-podcast-review),
[Adobe Podcast pricing](/blog/adobe-podcast-pricing) and
[Paraphrase vs Adobe Podcast](/blog/paraphrase-vs-adobe-podcast).

**Best for:** spoken recordings that need their sound cleaned up as much as their words cut, if you
work in desktop Chrome.

**Best for:** solo podcasts, voiceovers, narration and talking-head video where you want clean
cuts of your real voice and nothing else.

## Not text-based, but often on the same shortlist

### Cleanvoice

Cleanvoice's own FAQ says it "doesn't work based on the transcript-to-edit flow". It removes filler
words, stutters, mouth sounds, breaths, silences and noise automatically, so it pairs with a
text-based editor rather than replacing one. See our [Cleanvoice review](/blog/cleanvoice-review).

### Audacity

Audacity is free and open source (GPL v3), and you edit on the waveform. An optional Whisper plugin
transcribes into a label track, but its page does not describe editing audio through that
transcript, and its download page says the plugins are not yet compatible with the latest version
(the builds offered are for Audacity 3.7.1). See our [Audacity review](/blog/audacity-review).

## How to choose

Start with what happens before and after the edit, not the edit itself.

- **Do you record other people remotely?** Then the recording studio matters most. Riverside, or
  Descript's Rooms, or Podcastle (now Async), or Adobe Podcast Studio.
- **Is the output mostly video with captions and effects?** CapCut for social clips, Descript for
  longer pieces.
- **Need noise removal?** Every tool here except Paraphrase has some. Adobe Podcast's Enhance
  Speech has a free plan (up to 1 hour a day, files up to 30 minutes), and Cleanvoice is built
  around automatic clean-up.
- **Want generated speech impossible, not just switched off?** That is Paraphrase's defining choice.
- **Not English?** Check language lists. Descript's filler detection is English only, Podcastle
  lists five transcription languages and Adobe Podcast seven.
