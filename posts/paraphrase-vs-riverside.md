# Paraphrase vs Riverside: a recording studio versus a transcript editor

**Bottom line:** if you record guests remotely, use Riverside: it records each person locally on
their own device, gives you separate tracks, and its text-based editor, hosting and publishing are
built in. Paraphrase does none of the recording side. It is the better fit when you edit your own
voice and want an AI cleanup that proposes the whole edit in one pass, with a guarantee that the
export only contains words you actually said.

Riverside is a remote recording studio and content platform. Paraphrase is a browser editor for
your own recording. They overlap in one place, editing audio by editing its transcript, and that
is what this comparison is about.

## What Riverside's editing actually is

Riverside transcribes your recording and lets you edit by the words. From its help centre:

- Click a word or drag across a phrase, and delete it. Cut text stays in grey with a
  strikethrough.
- Copy and paste text to rearrange the recording.
- A filler word panel that groups "um", "uh", contextual fillers like "you know", breaths, and
  fillers that might clip a neighbouring word, each to accept or discard.
- Pause removal with a slider.
- Find Fluff, on paid plans, which suggests whole segments such as "redundant takes, chatter, and
  technical discussions" as comments.

That is a capable editor, and the word-level basics are close to what Paraphrase does.

## Where the approaches differ

**Generated speech.** Riverside offers tools that create audio you did not record: AI Voice
"simulates your speaking voice" from text, and VideoDub regenerates up to two words with lip
sync. Paraphrase **never generates speech**. There is no AI voice, clone, overdub or text to
speech. Every edit is a choice of which of your words stay and in what order, so a word you never
said cannot appear in an export. That is deliberate, and for some people it is the point.

**How the AI cleanup works.** Riverside documents separate passes: filler words, pauses, and Find
Fluff for segments. Paraphrase has one button. A language model reads the transcript and proposes
the cut: filled pauses, false starts, hedges and repetition, including the same point made twice
in different words, and it can move words where a sentence reads better. There is no word list;
it judges each word in context and keeps anything that carries meaning. The result is a proposal,
shown struck through, and any word can be put back.

**Where the AI runs.** Riverside says the third-party sub-processors it uses alongside its own AI
do not train on customer data. Paraphrase's cleanup runs on machines Paraphrase operates, and
nothing you submit is used to train any model.

The two plans buy very different things, so the prices are not a like-for-like saving. Plan by
plan figures are in the [Riverside pricing breakdown](/blog/riverside-pricing).

## What Paraphrase does not do

This list is long, and it should be:

- **No remote recording studio.** You can record yourself in the browser, but you cannot invite a
  guest.
- **No separate per-speaker tracks, and no speaker labels.**
- **No captions, subtitles or translation.**
- **No noise removal, enhancement, levelling, EQ, music or effects.**
- **No collaboration, comments or share links,** and no hosting or publishing.
- **The cleanup has been tested on English speech.**

If you need any of those, Riverside does them and Paraphrase does not.

## So which should you use?

Use **Riverside** if you interview people remotely, want each guest's audio recorded locally, or
want recording, editing, captions, hosting and publishing in one account. It is the more complete
product by a distance.

Use **Paraphrase** if you work alone (voiceovers, solo podcasts, course lessons, talking-head
video) and the edit is the job. See how it fits [podcast editing](/podcast-editing),
