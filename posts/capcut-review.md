# CapCut review: transcript editing and filler word removal for talking-head video

**Bottom line:** CapCut is a complete, free-to-start video editor, and its transcript-based
editing and filler word highlighting are a sensible way to tidy talking-head footage, with a review
step CapCut itself tells you to take seriously. Where it is less clear is what is free, what its
filler detection covers beyond ums and pauses, and the fact that the same transcript can also be
used to generate a cloned voice. If you want an editor that only ever cuts your own words,
Paraphrase does that one job; for everything else CapCut does, keep CapCut.

We make Paraphrase, a narrower tool that edits your recording by its transcript, so treat this as
an interested party's assessment. Every claim about CapCut below comes from CapCut's own pricing
page, help centre and feature pages, read on 17 October 2026. **We have not tested CapCut
hands-on for this review**, so we cannot tell you how accurate its transcripts are on your voice
or how its filler cuts sound. Where that matters, we say so.

## What CapCut is

CapCut is a video editor that runs on desktop (Windows and macOS), on mobile and in the browser.
It covers far more than talking-head editing: templates, effects and filters, transitions, sound
effects, auto captions, background removal, motion tracking, keyframe animation, green screen,
noise removal and voice enhancement, video translation, and a range of generative AI tools
including AI video generation, text to speech and AI voices. There is a free plan, and paid Pro,
Ultra and Teams plans. Our [CapCut pricing](/blog/capcut-pricing) post covers what those cost.

This review looks at one slice of it: editing video of yourself talking by editing the transcript.

## What CapCut does well

**Transcript-based editing is built into a full editor.** On desktop you select the clip and open
"Transcript-based editing" from the Layout menu; online you right-click the clip on the timeline.
CapCut transcribes it and, in its words, deleting transcript text removes the matching video. The
real advantage is what sits next to it: once the talking is cut, you are already in an editor with
captions, effects and templates, with no export and re-import between tools.

**Transcript edits carry into captions.** CapCut says "text corrections carry into the caption
track". For short-form video, where captions are close to mandatory, that is a genuine time
saver, and Paraphrase does not make captions at all.

**Filler words are highlighted, not silently deleted.** CapCut's desktop workflow starts in Auto
captions with an "Identify filter words" option (that is CapCut's spelling). It highlights
candidates such as "um", "uh", "like" and "you know" in the transcript, and you "choose whether to
delete or manually adjust them", with preview before export.

**CapCut's own guidance is honest.** Its article on filler word removal says to "Treat highlighting
as a shortlist, not a verdict", that "a detected word is not automatically a mistake", and that a
pause "is not automatically dead space". That is the right advice, and it is unusual to see a
vendor say it about its own feature.

**Export is flexible.** On desktop CapCut lets you set bit rate, codec, format, resolution and
frame rate, up to 4K and 60fps where your device and footage allow it, and share straight to
TikTok and YouTube.

## Where it runs out, or is unclear

**It is not clear what is free.** The transcript editing page calls it "free and online". The
filler words page says you can remove filler words "for free with CapCut's trial". A CapCut
article says auto captions, where the desktop filler workflow starts, are Pro only. Separately,
the help centre says any premium element in a project means Pro to export without a watermark, and
that on desktop the 4K, 60fps and No Watermark options are locked for non-Pro users. None of this
is unreasonable, but you may only find out what your edit needs when you try to export it.

**Filler detection is described as fillers and pauses.** CapCut's pages talk about ums, ahs,
"like", "you know" and pauses. We did not find them describing false starts, restarts, or the same
point made twice in different words. That does not mean it never catches them; it means CapCut
does not document it, and we have not tested it.

**Pause trimming needs checking.** CapCut says its filler removal "may also trim pauses" and
advises reviewing the timeline result. On talking-head video that is where a cut can feel abrupt,
so budget time to watch it back.

**The language list is unclear.** The transcript editing page claims "100+ languages", and the
same page's FAQ lists English, French, Russian and Arabic as the available options. Check yours
before you rely on it.

**The transcript can also generate speech.** CapCut has an AI overdub tool: "CapCut's advanced AI
voice cloning technology enables you to generate a precise and accurate version of your voice",
replacing "incorrect audio with accurate voice-over by editing the transcript". It is "only
available in certain regions". For many creators that is a feature. For anyone who needs the final
cut to contain only things they actually said, it is a reason to know which tool you are in.

**Billing has sharp edges.** The trial auto-renews, you need to cancel at least 24 hours before
renewal, and CapCut says purchases "generally" are not refunded because subscriptions "are
considered virtual goods".

## Where Paraphrase fits, and where it does not

Paraphrase is a browser app that does only the transcript part. You upload or record, it
transcribes with a timestamp on every word, and every edit is a choice of which of your words stay
and in what order.

- **It never generates speech.** No voice cloning, no overdub, no text to speech. A word you did
  not say cannot appear in the export, because there is no audio for it.
- **The cleanup is a proposal you review.** One button, and a language model reads the transcript
  and proposes cuts: filled pauses, false starts, hedges and repetition, including the same point
  made twice. It has no word list; it judges each word in context. Cuts show struck through and any
  word can be put back with a click. See [AI cleanup](/docs/ai-cleanup).
- **MP4 export cuts picture and sound at the same words.** For talking-head video, that is the
  whole job. Audio exports are WAV, FLAC, MP3, M4A and Opus. See [exporting](/docs/exporting).
- **Your transcript is not sent to a third-party model.** Cleanup runs on machines Paraphrase
  operates, and nothing you submit is used to train any model.

What Paraphrase does not do, plainly:

- **No captions or subtitles.** For short-form video that is a big gap, and CapCut fills it.
- **No effects, templates, music, transitions, noise removal or AI video.**
- **No mobile or desktop app.** It runs in the browser.
- **25 European languages** for transcription, and the cleanup is tested on English only.
- **It is paid beyond the free plan**: 15 minutes of transcription free, then Premium at £13.99 a
  month or about £4.99 a month on the three year cycle. See [pricing](/pricing).

## Who CapCut is right for

CapCut is right for you if you want one app that takes footage all the way to a finished,
captioned, styled short, on whatever device you have, and you are happy reviewing its filler
highlights by eye. For a lot of creators that is the whole answer, and the free plan is a fair
place to start.

It is worth adding something narrower if the slow part of your week is the talking itself: the
false starts, the second take of the same sentence, the point you made twice. That is where
Paraphrase earns its place, before CapCut rather than instead of it, for our
[short-form video](/short-form-video) and [video editing](/video-editing) use cases.
