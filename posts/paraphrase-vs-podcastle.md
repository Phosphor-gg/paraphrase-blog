# Paraphrase vs Podcastle: a whole studio versus one careful edit

**Bottom line:** Podcastle, now Async, is a complete podcast studio: remote recording on separate
tracks, a transcript and timeline editor, noise cleanup, AI voices and hosting. **Paraphrase** does
only the edit, but does it differently: a context-reading cleanup that proposes cutting false starts,
hedges and repetition as well as ums, words you can move as well as delete, 25 languages, and an
export that can only contain words you said. If you need the studio, use Podcastle. If you mostly
cut your own voice, Paraphrase is simpler and cheaper, and plenty of people could use both.

Podcastle has been renamed Async (podcastle.ai redirects to async.com, whose podcast page is headed
"Formerly Podcastle"). We use both names below.

## What Podcastle does

Async's podcast studio covers the whole job:

- **Record.** Remote recording with guests joining from a link, captured locally as separate tracks
  per speaker, up to 4K video and WAV audio, with a teleprompter and an iPhone app.
- **Edit.** A multitrack timeline, plus Text Mode, which transcribes your tracks and lets you remove a
  word from "both the transcript and the audio file".
- **Clean up.** A Filler tab that finds a preset list of fillers (mmm, um, uh, uhm, uhh, umm, mm,
  mhm), a Silences tab with an adjustable threshold, Rough Cut for video (filler words, retakes and
  silences), and Magic Dust for noise and levels.
- **Generate.** Over 1,000 AI voices in 15 languages, and voice cloning from a short sample.
- **Publish.** A Hosting Hub with an RSS feed and a podcast page.

That is a lot of genuinely useful tooling in one browser tab, and most of it Paraphrase does not have.

## Where the two edits differ

Both tools let you edit audio by editing a transcript. The differences are in how much of the edit
each one does for you, and what the export can contain.

**What the automatic cleanup looks for.** Podcastle's documented Filler tab searches for specific
filler sounds and silences over a threshold. Paraphrase's [Clean up with
AI](/docs/ai-cleanup) has no word list: a language model reads the transcript and proposes cutting
filled pauses, false starts, hedges and repetition, including the same point made twice in different
words, while keeping anything that carries meaning. It can also propose moving words where a sentence
reads better. Podcastle's Rough Cut, on video, also removes retakes; its documentation does not
describe how it decides, and we have not tested it, so we are not going to claim one is better on
your audio. Try both. Paraphrase's cleanup has been tested on English speech only.

**Silences.** Paraphrase has no silence remover. Its AI cleanup cuts words, and there is no "remove
every gap over half a second" button like Podcastle's. If long gaps are your main problem, Podcastle
handles them directly.

**What the export can contain.** Podcastle can generate speech: AI voices, and a cloned voice that
creates, in Async's words, "new speech you never actually recorded". Paraphrase **never generates
speech**. Its export is your original recording with pieces cut out at word boundaries, so a word you
never said cannot appear in it. If you want generated intros or sponsor reads, that is Podcastle's
strength. If you want certainty that the edit is only ever your own audio, that is Paraphrase's
whole design.

## What Paraphrase does not do

Being straight about this matters more than the table:

- No remote recording studio, multitrack, or separate per-speaker tracks.
- No noise removal, speech enhancement, levelling, EQ, music or sound effects.
- No speaker labels, captions, subtitles or translation.
- No collaboration, comments or share links, and no hosting.
- No AI voice, cloning, overdub or text to speech, ever.

If your show has remote guests and you publish from your editor, Podcastle covers ground Paraphrase
does not touch. The common setup where both make sense: record and host in Podcastle, and use
Paraphrase for the cut of your own narration or solo segments, then bring the result back. Paraphrase
exports WAV and FLAC, so a lossless master stays lossless.

## Why trust this comparison

Everything above about Podcastle comes from Async's own pages, all read on 17 October 2026: the
pricing page (prices shown to us in pounds), the podcast studio, recording, audio editor, Magic
Dust, filler word, text to speech and voice cloning pages, and help centre articles on transcription
languages, Text Mode, filler words, silence removal, Rough Cut and audio and video export. We have
not used it hands-on, so where a detail is not documented we say so rather than guess.

We make Paraphrase, so this is not a neutral comparison. What we have done instead is bold
Podcastle's wins in the table, give its strengths their own section, and leave out what we could
not read.

Where Async's own pages disagreed, we flagged it or left it out. The Essentials plan card says 4
hours of recording while the comparison table on the same page says 2 hours of remote multitrack
recording, so we quote neither. Its audio export article says "7 different audio formats" and then
names eight; the table lists the eight it names. The pricing table's per-plan checkmarks did not
come through when we read it, so we do not say which plan includes text-based editing or voice
cloning. We could not find the free trial length or when and why Podcastle became Async, and we
did not use the rating badges on its site because they named no source we could open.

**Where Paraphrase leads, and where it does not.** It never generates speech, so an export can only
contain words you said. Every AI cut is shown in the transcript and can be restored word by word.
Credits are spent only at transcription, so cleanup, re-editing and re-exporting are free. It is
also by far the narrower tool: no remote recording, no noise removal, no captions, no AI voice and
no collaboration.

We have no customer reviews, ratings or testimonials to show you, because we have not collected
any. Judge it on one of your own recordings instead.

## So which should you use?

**Use Podcastle** if you want one tool for recording guests, editing, cleaning up the sound, adding
AI voices and publishing. It is built for exactly that, and the paid plans buy a lot.

**Use Paraphrase** if you record yourself, spend most of your editing time cutting stumbles and
repetition, record in a language Podcastle does not list, or want an editor that cannot generate
your voice. More on the workflow in [podcast editing](/podcast-editing) and
[voiceover editing](/voiceover-editing).

Our [Podcastle review](/blog/podcastle-review) goes deeper on what it does well, and
[Podcastle alternatives](/blog/podcastle-alternatives) covers Descript, Riverside and the rest.

The fairest test is your own recording. Upload one to a [free Paraphrase
account](https://paraphrase.bot), press Clean up with AI, and compare it with what you would have cut
by hand. Fifteen minutes of transcription are included, no card needed.
