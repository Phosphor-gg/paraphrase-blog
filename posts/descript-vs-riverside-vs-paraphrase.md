# Descript vs Riverside vs Paraphrase: two production suites and one transcript editor

**Bottom line:** for most people this is a choice between Descript and Riverside, because both
record, edit by transcript, clean up the sound and add captions. Pick **Riverside** if you record
remote guests and want hosting, publishing and live streaming in the same account. Pick
**Descript** if you want desktop apps, screen recording, Studio Sound and Edit for Clarity. Pick
**Paraphrase** only if the job is cutting your own voice by its words: it has no recording studio
and no sound processing, and it never generates speech, but its cleanup proposes the whole cut
for you to review and re-editing never costs anything.

## What each one is for

**Descript** is an audio and video editor built around the transcript, with captions, screen
recording, Studio Sound, translation and dubbing, and Rooms, a browser studio where "Every
participant gets a separate file". It has macOS and Windows apps and a web version for Chromium
browsers.

**Riverside** starts from recording. Each guest's tracks "are recorded locally on each participant's
computer or device", so "Slow internet should not affect their quality." The editor adds captions,
Magic Audio, music and effects, and export to Premiere Pro or Final Cut Pro. Paid plans add podcast hosting, publishing to YouTube, Spotify, Apple and others, and
on Grow and above, live streaming and social scheduling.

**Paraphrase** does one thing: you record or upload, every word gets a timestamp, and you edit by
choosing which of your words stay and in what order. The export is your original recording with
pieces cut out at word boundaries.

## Where the three overlap

All three let you cut by deleting words from a transcript and see what you cut. Descript keeps
removed text as "ignored text" you can restore; Riverside shows it "in gray with a strikethrough";
Paraphrase leaves cut words on screen, struck through, and one click puts each back. Riverside lets
you copy and paste text to rearrange the recording; Paraphrase lets you drag a word to move it.

- **Descript** has Remove filler words, which lists each filler with a timestamp and preview and
  "currently detects filler words in English transcripts only", and Edit for Clarity, which cleans
  "up pacing" and removes "unnecessary or off-topic content". Both use AI credits.
- **Riverside** has a filler panel covering "um", "uh" and "hmm", contextual fillers like "you
  know" that are "flagged only when used as a filler", breaths, and a pause slider. On paid plans,
  Find Fluff suggests whole segments such as "redundant takes, chatter, and technical discussions".
- **Paraphrase** has one button. A language model reads the transcript and proposes the cut: filled
  pauses, false starts, hedges and repetition, including the same point made twice in different
  words, and it can move words where a sentence reads better. There is no word list. It is a
  proposal you review word by word. It has been tested on English speech. See
  [how AI cleanup works](/docs/ai-cleanup).

We have not compared output quality side by side, so this describes approaches, not a winner.

**Generated speech** is the clearest split. Descript's Regenerate can "generate text-to-speech audio
to fix a flubbed delivery", and Riverside's AI Voice "simulates your speaking voice" while VideoDub
regenerates up to two words with lip sync. Both gate it: the voice has to be authorised before it is used. Paraphrase **never generates speech**. A word you never said cannot appear in
an export, because there is no audio for it. More on why in
[why Paraphrase never generates speech](/blog/why-paraphrase-never-generates-speech).

nothing, so re-editing a recording or exporting it again is free. See [pricing](/pricing).

## What Paraphrase does not do

Both competitors do most of this:

- No remote recording studio, multitrack or separate per-speaker tracks.
- No noise removal, enhancement, levelling, EQ, music or sound effects.
- No speaker labels, captions, subtitles or translation.
- No hosting, publishing, collaboration, comments or share links.
- No screen recording, no desktop or mobile app, no public API.
- Transcription in 25 European languages, where Riverside lists around a hundred.

## Which should you pick?

**You interview guests remotely and publish a show:** Riverside. Local recording per guest, hosting
and publishing in one account. Our [Riverside review](/blog/riverside-review) goes deeper.

**You make tutorials, screen recordings or video essays:** Descript. Screen recording, Studio Sound
and Edit for Clarity sit in the same editor. See our [Descript review](/blog/descript-review).

**You record alone and the edit is the slow part:** Paraphrase. A solo
[podcast](/podcast-editing), [voiceover](/voiceover-editing) or [course](/course-editing) where the
work is cutting restarts and repetition, with a WAV or FLAC export that stays lossless.

**You edit interviews, testimony or a client's voice and must not add words:** Paraphrase, because
the guarantee comes from the tool rather than a setting you remember to leave off.

**You already pay for Descript or Riverside:** keep it. The pairwise posts,
[Paraphrase vs Descript](/blog/paraphrase-vs-descript) and
[Paraphrase vs Riverside](/blog/paraphrase-vs-riverside), cover each in more depth.
