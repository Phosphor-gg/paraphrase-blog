# Riverside alternatives: the honest options for editing your voice

**Bottom line:** if you record remote interviews and need separate, locally recorded tracks for
every guest, only **Descript** (Rooms) and **Adobe Podcast** (Studio) on this list also record
that way, and staying with Riverside is often the simplest answer. If you mostly record yourself
and Riverside is really your editor, **Paraphrase** is a narrower tool that edits your own
recording by its transcript, never generates speech, and has an AI cleanup that proposes the whole
edit in one pass. **Descript** is the closest all-round match if you want remote recording and
text editing plus AI voices.

Riverside started as a remote recording studio and has grown into a platform: recording, a
text-based editor, AI tools, podcast hosting, live streaming, webinars and newsletters. People
looking for alternatives usually want one of three things, and only some of them are solved by
switching.

## Why people look for Riverside alternatives

**They do not use the studio.** Riverside's headline feature is recording guests remotely. Its
help centre explains that "High-quality tracks are recorded locally on each participant's computer
or device. Slow internet should not affect their quality." That is genuinely valuable for
interviews. If you record solo voiceovers, course lessons or monologue episodes, you are paying
for a studio you do not open.

**The price is set by what the platform does.** Pro was £35 a month, or £24 a month billed
yearly, on Riverside's pricing page as shown to a UK visitor on 17 October 2026. That buys 4K
recording, hosting, publishing, AI B-roll and more. If you only want to cut ums out of your own voice, that
is a lot of platform. We have covered [what Riverside costs](/blog/riverside-pricing) in detail.

**They want different AI behaviour.** Riverside offers AI Voice, voice clones and VideoDub, which
generate speech in your voice. Some people want that. Others want an editor that cannot put words
in their mouth.

On generated speech: Riverside has AI Voice, voice clones and VideoDub, Descript has AI speech and
voice clones, and Paraphrase never generates speech. We kept each tool's entry to what its own
pages said when we read them on 17 October 2026.

## Like-for-like alternatives to Riverside

### Descript

The closest all-round match. Descript's pricing page describes text-based editing, a Remove
Filler Words tool, and AI speech with custom voice clones on its paid plans, with a free plan to
start. It also records remotely: its Rooms page says "Everyone records locally in up to 4K" and
"Every participant gets a separate file". If what you like about Riverside is recording guests and
editing by the transcript, and you also want generated speech for fixing lines, Descript covers
that.

**Best for:** people who want an editor first and are happy with AI voice in the toolbox.

### Adobe Podcast

The other one here, besides Descript, that records remote guests. In Adobe Podcast's Studio
(labelled beta), you share a link, each participant's audio "is captured directly in their browser
and continuously saved", and you get "separate, high-quality tracks for each speaker" after the
session. Studio transcribes the recording and you edit by deleting text from the transcript. Its
other tool, Enhance Speech, removes background noise and echo. There is a free plan with daily
limits; downloading the original speaker-separated recordings needs the paid plan, which for new
customers is now Adobe's Firefly Pro, shown at $19.99 a month on podcast.adobe.com on
17 October 2026. Studio is only supported in Chrome on desktop.

**Best for:** people who want remote recording and noise clean-up in the browser.

## Alternatives that do one job

### Audacity

Free, open source, runs on Windows, macOS and Linux. It edits a waveform, not a transcript, so
every cut is done by eye and ear. Its site says it "has always been and will always remain free
for everyone".

**Best for:** people who prefer manual control and do not want a subscription.

### Paraphrase

Paraphrase is what we make, so weigh this accordingly.

It edits **your own recording by editing its transcript**. You record in the browser or upload
audio or video, every word gets a timestamp, and every edit is a choice of which of your words
stay and in what order. Click a word to cut it, drag a word to move it. The export is your
original recording with pieces cut out, joined at word boundaries, as WAV, FLAC, MP3, M4A or Opus,
or MP4 when the project has a video take.

The difference that matters most against Riverside: **it never generates speech.** No AI voice,
no voice clone, no overdub. A word you never said cannot appear in an export because there is no
audio for it. That is a design decision, not a missing feature.

The other difference is the AI cleanup. Riverside documents separate tools for filler words,
pauses and, on paid plans, Find Fluff, which suggests whole segments to remove. Paraphrase has one
button: a language model reads the transcript and proposes the cut, covering filled pauses, false
starts, hedges and repetition, including the same point made twice in different words, and it can
move words where a sentence reads better. Every cut shows struck through and can be put back. It
runs on machines Paraphrase operates, and the transcript is not sent to a third-party model. It
has been tested on English speech.

Pricing is £13.99 a month, about £6.99 a month on the annual cycle or about £4.99 on the three
year cycle, with 120 hours of audio a month. Editing, cleanup and export use no credits. There is
a [full breakdown on the pricing page](/pricing).

It is **not a Riverside replacement** if you record guests. No remote recording studio, no
separate per-speaker tracks, no speaker labels, no captions, no noise removal, no collaboration
and no hosting. If you interview guests remotely, you need something that records them.

**Best for:** solo voice work, where the edit is the job. See how it fits
[podcast editing](/podcast-editing) or [voiceover editing](/voiceover-editing).

## What you will struggle to replace

Worth being straight about: **Riverside's local recording is the thing most alternatives here do
not do** (Descript and Adobe Podcast are the exceptions). Each participant's track is recorded on
their own device, and Riverside's help centre says "Slow internet should not affect their
quality." You can download those separate tracks as WAV or MP4 for another editor, 15 hours a
month on Pro.

It is also a platform. Hosting, publishing to YouTube and Spotify, live streaming, webinars,
Magic Clips and show notes all live in one account. If you use several of those, switching means
replacing several tools, not one.

## Do you actually need to replace Riverside?

Often not. A common setup is to keep Riverside for recording guests and use a narrower tool where
it suits you better. If your episodes are solo, though, or you mostly upload recordings made
elsewhere, you may be paying for capabilities you never touch.
